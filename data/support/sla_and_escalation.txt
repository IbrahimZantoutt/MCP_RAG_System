1. PURPOSE

This document defines what Helix commits to when a customer has a problem, how
fast we must respond, who gets involved at each stage, and when a customer is
entitled to a service credit.

It was substantially revised following incident INC-2024-017. The revisions
concern availability measurement, detection obligations, and credit calculation.
Where this document differs from the 2023 policy, the difference is deliberate
and is noted in section 9.

3. SEVERITY DEFINITIONS

Severity is set by Helix support based on observed impact, not by the customer's
assertion. Customers may dispute a severity assignment and that dispute is
resolved by the Director of Customer Support.

SEV-1 — CRITICAL
  Fleet-wide outage, or throughput loss exceeding 50 percent, or any safety-
  relevant event, or any condition preventing the customer from meeting a
  committed shipping window.
  Examples: Fleet OS unreachable; more than half the fleet unavailable; any
  robot behaving unexpectedly in a way that could endanger personnel; the
  charge dock reservation deadlock signature described in Fleet Recovery Runbook
  section 4.

SEV-2 — MAJOR
  Significant degradation not meeting SEV-1 thresholds. Throughput loss between
  15 and 50 percent, or loss of a major function, or a defect with no workaround
  that materially impacts operations.

SEV-3 — MINOR
  Limited impact. A workaround exists. Individual robot faults, cosmetic
  defects, isolated task failures.

SEV-4 — INFORMATIONAL
  Questions, documentation requests, feature enquiries, planned change
  coordination.

Severity escalates automatically. Any SEV-2 unresolved after 8 hours escalates
to SEV-1. Any SEV-3 unresolved after 5 business days escalates to SEV-2.


4. ESCALATION MATRIX

  Level 1   Support Engineer (on duty)
            Owns initial triage, runbook execution, known-issue resolution.
            Escalates within 30 minutes for SEV-1 if not resolved.

  Level 2   Field Operations (Marcus Ozturk's group)
            Owns on-site dispatch, hardware diagnosis, customer-site
            coordination. First line for customer sites. Engineering is not the
            first voice on the phone with a customer and should not try to be.

  Level 3   Engineering on-call
            Primary responds within 15 minutes for SEV-1, 1 hour for SEV-2.
            Secondary is backup. Rotation covers Fleet Software, Firmware, and
            Platform.

  Level 4   Group lead / CTO
            Engaged for any SEV-1 exceeding 2 hours, any incident affecting
            multiple customer sites, or any safety-relevant event without
            exception.

Additional Enterprise obligations
  - TAM notified at SEV-1 declaration regardless of hour.
  - Named executive sponsor notified for any SEV-1 exceeding 4 hours.
  - Written incident summary delivered to the customer within 5 business days of
    resolution.


5. AVAILABILITY MEASUREMENT

This section changed materially in the 2024 revision.

Fleet availability is measured monthly per site as:

    availability = (fleet-minutes available) / (fleet-minutes scheduled)

where a robot-minute counts as available if the robot is powered, connected, not
faulted, AND eligible for task dispatch.

The eligibility clause is the change. Under the 2023 policy, availability counted
robots that were powered, connected, and unfaulted. During INC-2024-017 the 61
deadlocked robots satisfied all three of those conditions perfectly — they were
powered, connected, and reported no fault — while being completely unable to do
any work. Under the old definition, the worst outage in company history measured
as near-perfect availability. That was indefensible, and Cardinal Foods said so.

A robot sitting at a charging dock holding a reservation it will never release is
not available. A robot that is charging on a normal cycle is not counted against
scheduled fleet-minutes at all, because charging is expected. The distinction is
between planned unavailability, which is excluded from the denominator, and
unplanned ineligibility, which counts as downtime.

Excluded from availability calculation:
  - Scheduled maintenance windows agreed with the customer at least 5 business
    days in advance
  - Normal charge cycles within the site's provisioned dock capacity
  - Customer-caused outages: facility power loss, customer network failure,
    customer WMS unavailability, facility Wi-Fi below the contracted RF site
    requirement
  - Force majeure

Not excluded, and this is deliberate:
  - Any Helix software defect, including defects arising from interactions
    between individually correct versions
  - Any condition where robots are ineligible for dispatch for reasons under
    Helix control
  - Detection delay. If we did not notice, the clock still runs.


6. SERVICE CREDITS — INTERNAL

Credits are calculated against the monthly software licence fee for the affected
site, not against hardware or total contract value.

  Availability below commitment but above commitment minus 0.5 pp   10 percent
  Below commitment minus 0.5 pp, above commitment minus 2.0 pp      25 percent
  Below commitment minus 2.0 pp                                     50 percent
  Any single SEV-1 exceeding 6 hours                                additional
                                                                    25 percent

Credits are capped at 100 percent of the monthly licence fee for the affected
site. Credits are applied to the following invoice and are not paid in cash.

Credit assessment is performed by Support and approved by the CFO. Support staff
must not discuss credit eligibility with customers. That conversation belongs to
the account team, always, without exception. A well-meaning support engineer
speculating about credits creates a commitment we may not be able to honour.

INC-2024-017 worked example, recorded here as the reference case:
  Site: Cardinal Foods Memphis, 240 units, Enterprise tier
  Monthly licence: 240 x 265 USD = 63,600 USD
  Duration: 6 hours 47 minutes of degraded operation, peak 71 percent
            throughput loss
  Measured September availability under the revised definition: 98.7 percent
  Commitment: 99.9 percent. Shortfall: 1.2 pp, placing it in the 25 percent band
  SEV-1 exceeding 6 hours: additional 25 percent
  Total: 50 percent of monthly licence...

  The assessed credit was 148,000 USD rather than the formula figure of
  31,800 USD. The difference is a negotiated goodwill credit approved by Rowan
  Achebe and Sam Delacroix, extending across the full quarter's licence fees for
  the Memphis site plus a contribution toward the customer's expedited freight
  costs on the two missed trailers. This was a commercial decision to protect a
  renewal, not a contractual entitlement, and it should not be treated as
  precedent. See the Cardinal Foods account record for the commercial context.


7. DETECTION OBLIGATIONS — INTERNAL

Added in the 2024 revision. This has no customer-facing equivalent and creates
no contractual obligation, but it is how we hold ourselves accountable.

For every incident, support must record the detection method:
  A. Helix automated alerting
  B. Helix staff observation
  C. Customer report

Any SEV-1 or SEV-2 detected by method C is automatically treated as a monitoring
defect and raised with SRE, regardless of how well the subsequent response went.

During INC-2024-017 the detection gap was 41 minutes and the notification
arrived by telephone from a Cardinal Foods shift supervisor. Our response after
that point was fast and comfortably inside the 1-hour Enterprise commitment. It
did not matter. The customer knew before we did, and that single fact drove more
change than anything else in the postmortem — including the three new fleet-level
alerting rules shipped in Fleet OS 4.2.2.

The standard we hold: the customer is not our monitoring.


8. INCIDENT DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS — INTERNAL

  - Every SEV-1 requires a written postmortem within 5 business days. Use the
    INC-2024-017 postmortem as the structural template.
  - Every SEV-2 exceeding 4 hours requires a written summary.
  - Postmortems are blameless. Blameless does not mean vague — name the exact
    code, the exact mechanism, the exact process gap. Do not name an individual
    as the cause.
  - Every postmortem must record detection method per section 7.
  - Action items require a named owner and a date. The INC-2024-017 postmortem
    carried nine action items; eight were complete within eight weeks and the
    ninth was formally deferred to Fleet OS 5.0 as tracked architectural debt.
    Formal deferral is acceptable. Silent drift is not.


9. CHANGES FROM THE 2023 POLICY

  - Availability now requires dispatch eligibility, not merely power, connection,
    and absence of faults (section 5). Direct consequence of INC-2024-017.
  - Detection method recording is now mandatory, and customer-detected SEV-1 and
    SEV-2 events are automatically monitoring defects (section 7).
  - Enterprise critical response tightened from 2 hours to 1 hour.
  - Added the additional 25 percent credit band for any single SEV-1 exceeding
    6 hours (section 6).
  - Added explicit exclusion for facility Wi-Fi below the contracted RF site
    requirement, after repeated disputes where coverage gaps at customer sites
    were attributed to robot performance.
  - Clarified that support staff must not discuss credit eligibility with
    customers under any circumstances.

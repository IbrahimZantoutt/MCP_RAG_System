HELIX ROBOTICS — CUSTOMER FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Owner: Aisha Bello, Director of Customer Support
Last revised: 2024-12-08
Classification: Customer-facing. This document may be shared freely with
                customers and prospects.


GENERAL

What does Helix Fleet OS actually do?
  Fleet OS is the software that coordinates your robots. It decides which robot
  does which task, routes them through shared floor space without collisions or
  deadlocks, schedules charging, and connects to your warehouse management
  system. One Fleet OS instance manages one facility.

Does Fleet OS run in the cloud?
  No. Fleet OS runs on an appliance in your facility. This is deliberate. A
  network partition between your site and the internet must never be able to
  stop your robots from working. We export telemetry to Helix Cloud for support
  and analytics, but that path is one-way and non-critical; if it fails, your
  fleet keeps running normally.

What happens if the Fleet OS server fails?
  Robots complete their current task and hold position. Nothing moves unsafely.
  The system fails static by design. Restarting the service takes under a minute,
  with a planning pause of 20 to 40 seconds. No durable data is lost and robots
  do not need to be re-homed.

Can one Fleet OS instance manage multiple buildings?
  No. Each facility runs its own isolated instance. Instances do not share floor
  space or communicate with each other.

How many robots can one instance handle?
  Up to 400. Our largest production deployment is 240 HX-200 units at a single
  customer site.


SAFETY

Are the robots safe to operate around people?
  Yes. Both the HX-200 Courier and HX-450 Stacker are certified for continuous
  mixed-traffic operation — robots and people sharing the same floor — under
  ISO 3691-4 and ANSI/RIA R15.08, with the safety scanner chain certified to
  IEC 61508 SIL 2.

Can the fleet software override a safety function?
  No, and this is architectural rather than a policy choice. All safety functions
  live in robot firmware and in safety-rated hardware. Fleet OS tells a robot
  where to go; firmware decides how to get there and retains absolute authority
  to refuse or abort any command that would violate a safety constraint. Fleet OS
  cannot override an emergency stop, disable obstacle detection, or command
  motion outside the certified speed envelope. This is a certification
  requirement and is not configurable, including for testing.

What happens if a robot loses connectivity or gets confused about its position?
  It performs a controlled stop and holds position. Robots never continue under
  uncertainty.

How do we stop the whole fleet in an emergency?
  Every robot has physical emergency stop buttons on all four chassis faces (the
  HX-450 adds one at mast height), and a wireless operator pendant triggers a
  facility-wide stop. You can also pause the fleet from the Operator Console,
  though for genuine emergencies use the physical or pendant e-stop.


OPERATIONS

How long do the robots run on a charge?
  The HX-200 Courier runs about 8.5 hours on a typical duty cycle, or about 6
  hours under continuous heavy duty. Charging from 20 to 90 percent takes about
  75 minutes. The HX-450 Stacker runs about 7 hours typical and takes about 110
  minutes to charge from 20 to 90 percent.

How many charging docks do we need?
  One dock per four HX-200 units, and one dock per three HX-450 units. The
  Stacker ratio is higher because it charges more slowly and runs for less time.
  Under-provisioning docks is the most common site design error we see, and it
  produces a chronic throughput shortfall that is easy to mistake for a robot
  performance problem.

Do robots decide when to charge, or does the software?
  Fleet OS decides. Robots report their state of charge; the Charge Scheduler
  decides when each robot stops working, which dock it uses, and when it returns
  to the floor. The scheduler balances keeping enough robots on the floor to meet
  your throughput against never letting a robot run too low to reach a dock.

What happens during a Fleet OS upgrade?
  A planning pause of 20 to 40 seconds. Robots hold position and resume
  automatically. We recommend scheduling upgrades at a shift boundary.

Do we have to take every software update?
  No. We maintain a long-term-support firmware line for customers with formal
  change control requirements, receiving security and critical fixes only.
  Several regulated customers run on LTS deliberately. Your TAM can advise on
  which line suits your change management process.

Why do Fleet OS and firmware have different version numbers?
  They are released independently, because robot firmware and fleet software
  change at genuinely different rates. We support any Fleet OS release against
  firmware from the preceding twelve months, and since November 2024 every
  supported combination is verified nightly by an automated cross-version test
  matrix rather than by documentation alone.


TROUBLESHOOTING

Our robots are queuing at the chargers and not doing work. What is happening?
  Contact support. This can indicate a charge dock scheduling problem, and it is
  worth telling you plainly that we had a serious defect of exactly this shape in
  September 2024: on Fleet OS 4.2.0 and 4.2.1 combined with HX-200 firmware
  3.8.1, robots could hold a charging dock reservation that was never released,
  while reporting themselves perfectly healthy. It is fixed in Fleet OS 4.2.2 and
  firmware 3.8.2, both of which are required. If you are on any earlier
  combination, contact your TAM about upgrading. Fleet OS 4.2.3 also adds a
  one-click action to clear stale dock reservations.

A robot has stopped and shows an error code. What do I do?
  Your facility operators have the Troubleshooting Guide, which covers each code
  with diagnostic steps. The most common codes are E-207 (LiDAR occlusion,
  usually resolved by cleaning the sensor window), E-330 (WMS sync failure,
  usually a credential or certificate expiry on your side), and E-115 (emergency
  stop engaged).

We keep getting E-207 on multiple robots.
  Usually airborne dust. Cleaning sensor windows resolves most cases. If the rate
  is rising across many robots, it generally reflects facility air quality rather
  than robot failures. Firmware 3.9.0 lets E-207 clear automatically once the
  obstruction is gone, which substantially reduces the operational nuisance;
  earlier firmware requires manual acknowledgement.

Tasks have stopped arriving from our WMS.
  That is E-330. The most common causes are expired credentials, expired TLS
  certificates, or a change to your WMS. We cannot rotate your credentials, so
  these usually need your IT team. Open a ticket and we will work it jointly.

A robot stopped and says it does not know where it is.
  That is E-611. The usual cause is that something in the facility moved without
  the map being updated — racking changes, seasonal displays, temporary staging.
  Tell us what changed and we will update the map. If several robots report it in
  the same area, it is almost certainly a map issue rather than a robot issue.


SUPPORT AND SERVICE LEVELS

What are the support tiers?
  Standard (99.0 percent availability, next-business-day critical response),
  Professional (99.5 percent, 4-hour critical response 24x7, named support
  engineer), and Enterprise (99.9 percent, 1-hour critical response 24x7, named
  Technical Account Manager, quarterly business review, 24-hour on-site response
  for critical issues where remote resolution has failed).

How exactly do you measure availability?
  A robot counts as available if it is powered, connected, not faulted, and
  eligible to be assigned work. That last condition matters: a robot that is
  online and unfaulted but unable to do any work is not available.

  We tightened this definition in October 2024. Under our previous definition, a
  robot sitting idle at a charging dock while unable to accept tasks would still
  have counted as available. An incident in September 2024 showed us that this
  allowed a serious operational failure to measure as near-perfect availability,
  which was not a defensible position. We changed the definition against our own
  commercial interest because the old one was wrong.

  Normal charging cycles within your provisioned dock capacity are excluded from
  the calculation entirely, since charging is expected. Scheduled maintenance
  agreed at least five business days in advance is also excluded, as are outages
  caused by facility power loss, your network, your WMS, or facility Wi-Fi below
  the contracted coverage requirement.

What if you miss the availability commitment?
  Service credits apply against the monthly software licence fee for the affected
  site, on a banded scale, with an additional band for any single critical
  incident exceeding six hours. Your account team will walk you through the
  specifics in your agreement.

Will you tell us what went wrong when something breaks?
  Yes. Every critical incident gets a written postmortem within five business
  days, and for Enterprise customers that is a contractual commitment. Our
  postmortems name the actual mechanism — the specific code, the specific
  interaction, the specific process gap. We would rather tell you exactly what
  happened than give you a summary that protects us.

Do you notify us, or do we notify you?
  We should notify you. We record the detection method for every incident, and
  any significant incident that a customer reported before our monitoring caught
  it is automatically treated internally as a monitoring defect, regardless of
  how well we handled the response afterward. The standard we hold ourselves to
  is that the customer is not our monitoring.


CAPABILITIES WE DO NOT HAVE

Asked often enough to be worth stating plainly.

  Outdoor or yard operation. Our robots are IP54 and certified for indoor use
  only. This is not a configuration option.

  High-bay racking. The HX-450 reaches 1,800 mm on the base mast and 2,400 mm
  with the extended mast. We do not serve high-bay today.

  Single sign-on for the Operator Console. Authentication is currently local to
  Fleet OS. SAML and OIDC SSO with role-based access control and a tamper-evident
  audit log is scheduled for the first half of 2025.

  EU data residency for telemetry. Telemetry currently exports to US regions.
  EU residency is scheduled for the second half of 2025.

  Automatic failover between redundant Fleet OS servers. Failover is manual by
  design. Two controllers that both believe they own the floor is a considerably
  worse outcome than a brief cutover performed by a person who has confirmed the
  primary is genuinely down.

  Third-party robot support. Fleet OS manages Helix robots only.

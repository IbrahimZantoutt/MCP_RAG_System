HELIX ROBOTICS — SUPPORT TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE
Fault codes and diagnostic procedures
Owner: Aisha Bello, Director of Customer Support
Technical review: Tobias Lindqvist (Firmware), Marcus Ozturk (Field Operations)
Applies to: HX-200 firmware 3.7.0+, HX-450 firmware 2.4.0+, Fleet OS 4.2.x
Last revised: 2024-12-01
Classification: Internal — may be shared with customer facility staff


HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Each fault code entry gives the meaning, the common causes in rough order of
frequency, the diagnostic steps, and the resolution. Follow the steps in order.

This guide covers individual faults. If you are looking at a fleet-level problem
— many robots affected, throughput declining, no individual fault codes present
— this is the wrong document. Go to the Fleet Recovery Runbook. In particular,
robots queuing at chargers while reporting healthy is runbook section 4, not a
fault code, because the defining characteristic of that condition is the
*absence* of a fault code.

SAFETY. Nothing in this guide requires entering the robot operating area while
robots are live. Where floor access is needed, the procedure says so explicitly
and the fleet must be paused and the zone locked out first per the Field
Technician Training Manual.

E-115  —  EMERGENCY STOP ENGAGED
=============================================================================

Meaning
  A physical or wireless emergency stop has been triggered. The robot is stopped
  and will not move until the stop is cleared and reset.

Common causes
  1. Deliberate operator action
  2. Physical e-stop button knocked or pressed accidentally, most often by a
     passing pallet jack or by contact with racking
  3. Wireless pendant e-stop triggered facility-wide
  4. E-stop button mechanically stuck

Diagnostics
  - Determine which e-stop is engaged: helixctl robot detail <ROBOT_ID>
  - If facility-wide, check whether the pendant e-stop is latched
  - Establish why before clearing. An e-stop that was pressed deliberately was
    pressed for a reason and someone knows what it was.

Resolution
  - Confirm the area is clear and the reason for the stop is resolved
  - Physically twist-release the engaged button, or release the pendant
  - Reset from the Operator Console or the robot's reset control
  - On firmware earlier than 3.8.0, a race in the reset path could require two
    physical presses. Fixed in 3.8.0. On hardware revision A units with firmware
    earlier than 3.7.1, E-115 could fail to clear on the console after a
    successful physical reset; fixed in 3.7.1.

Escalate if
  The e-stop cannot be cleared after physical reset on current firmware, which
  suggests a hardware fault in the e-stop chain. This is safety-relevant. Take
  the robot out of service and escalate to Level 3 immediately.

E-118  —  PAYLOAD SHIFT DETECTED
=============================================================================

Meaning
  IMU signature analysis detected the load moving during transit. The robot has
  halted to prevent losing the load.

Common causes
  1. Improperly seated tote or container
  2. Overloaded or unevenly loaded tote
  3. Load exceeding rated payload
  4. Floor irregularity — expansion joint, damaged floor, debris

Diagnostics
  - Visually inspect the load (fleet paused, zone locked out)
  - Check reported payload mass: helixctl robot detail <ROBOT_ID> --telemetry
  - Check whether the event location correlates with a known floor issue. Repeat
    E-118 events at the same coordinates almost always mean a floor problem, not
    a loading problem.

Resolution
  - Reseat or redistribute the load, acknowledge the fault, resume
  - If location-correlated, raise a facility floor remediation request with the
    customer. Floor flatness is a customer-side site requirement.

Notes
  E-118 was introduced in firmware 3.8.0 following three tote spill events at
  Cardinal Foods in Q2 2024. It is a safety feature and a nuisance-fault source
  in roughly equal measure during the first weeks after a site enables it. A
  spike in E-118 immediately after a firmware upgrade to 3.8.0 or later is
  expected and usually reflects pre-existing loading practice rather than a new
  problem.

E-207  —  LIDAR OCCLUSION
=============================================================================

Meaning
  A navigation or safety LiDAR is obstructed or excessively dirty and cannot
  return reliable data.

Common causes
  1. Dust or debris accumulation on the sensor window — by far the most common
  2. Airborne dust, particularly in grocery and dry goods facilities
  3. Physical obstruction: shrink wrap, label, packaging debris
  4. Sensor hardware failure

Diagnostics
  - Identify which scanner: helixctl robot detail <ROBOT_ID>
  - Check whether the fault is persistent or intermittent. Intermittent
    strongly suggests airborne dust; persistent suggests physical obstruction or
    hardware failure.
  - Check the site's E-207 rate over time. A rising trend across many robots
    indicates a facility air quality change, not robot failures.

Resolution
  - Clean the sensor window with the supplied lens cloth. This resolves the
    large majority of cases.
  - On firmware 3.9.0 and later, E-207 self-clears after 3 consecutive clean
    scans. On earlier firmware it latches and requires manual acknowledgement —
    a significant nuisance at dusty sites and one of the main reasons to upgrade.
  - Persistent E-207 after cleaning means scanner replacement. Safety scanners
    are safety-rated components; replacement requires a Helix field technician
    and post-replacement verification. Customer staff must not replace them.

Notes
  Cardinal Foods Memphis historically generated a high E-207 rate from airborne
  dust. Firmware 3.7.1 improved transient occlusion recovery and 3.9.0 added
  self-clearing, which together reduced their E-207 ticket volume by roughly
  70 percent.

E-330  —  WMS SYNC FAILURE
=============================================================================

Meaning
  The Site Adapter cannot exchange data with the customer's warehouse management
  system. Surfaced on robots because it stops task flow, but the fault is in
  Fleet OS, not in the robot.

Common causes
  1. Expired customer WMS credentials
  2. Expired TLS certificate on the WMS endpoint
  3. Customer WMS upgrade changing message schema
  4. Customer network change, firewall rule, or endpoint address change
  5. Customer WMS unavailable

Diagnostics
  Full procedure is Fleet Recovery Runbook section 6. In brief:
  - helixctl adapter status
  - helixctl adapter test-connection
  - journalctl -u helix-site-adapter --since "1 hour ago"

Resolution
  - Credential and certificate expiry require customer IT. Helix cannot rotate
    customer WMS credentials. Engage the TAM and the customer integration
    contact.
  - Schema changes require engineering. Capture a failing message sample and
    escalate with it attached. Never hand-edit adapter mappings in production.

Notes
  E-330 is the highest-volume support category by ticket count. The cause is on
  the customer side in the substantial majority of cases, which makes tone
  important: the goal is to get the customer's IT team engaged quickly, not to
  win an argument about whose fault it is. Availability impact from
  customer-caused WMS outages is excluded from SLA measurement.

E-401  —  CHARGE RESERVATION TIMEOUT
=============================================================================

Meaning
  A robot's charging dock reservation expired before it completed charging, or
  the robot could not reach its reserved dock in time.

Common causes
  1. Robot blocked en route to the dock
  2. Dock occupied by an obstruction or a manually parked robot
  3. Dock hardware fault preventing handshake
  4. Insufficient dock capacity for fleet size

Diagnostics
  - helixctl dock status
  - Check the robot's path history to the dock
  - Check dock queue depth: helixctl dock queue

Resolution
  - Clear physical obstructions from the dock and approach path
  - Verify dock firmware. Handshake failures occur on docks running dock
    firmware older than 1.4; update the dock.
  - If E-401 is widespread, evaluate dock provisioning. Recommended ratios are
    one dock per four HX-200 units and one per three HX-450 units. Under-
    provisioning is the most common Stacker site design error.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
  E-401 is a timeout — the system noticed a problem and reported it. This is the
  healthy failure mode.

  The dangerous condition is the opposite: a robot holding a dock reservation
  with NO fault code at all, reporting itself perfectly healthy, forever. That
  is the INC-2024-017 signature and it produces no E-401 and no alert. On Fleet
  OS 4.2.0 and 4.2.1 dock reservations had no expiry, so nothing could ever time
  out. If you are seeing robots accumulate at docks with no fault codes and
  declining fleet availability, go to Fleet Recovery Runbook section 4 and check
  versions first. Fixed in Fleet OS 4.2.2 with firmware 3.8.2; both are required.

E-502  —  BATTERY THERMAL CUTOFF
=============================================================================

Meaning
  Battery temperature exceeded safe operating range. Charging and high-current
  discharge are inhibited until temperature returns to range.

Common causes
  1. High ambient temperature. HX-200 operating range is 0 C to 40 C ambient.
  2. Sustained heavy duty cycle with insufficient rest
  3. Blocked ventilation
  4. Battery cell degradation near end of service life
  5. Spurious reports on firmware 3.8.0 and earlier above 32 C ambient — fixed
     in 3.8.1

Diagnostics
  - helixctl robot detail <ROBOT_ID> --telemetry, check battery temperature and
    cell voltage deviation
  - Check facility ambient temperature in the robot's operating zone
  - Check cumulative charge cycle count. Rated life is 4,000 cycles to 80
    percent capacity, roughly 5 years at typical duty.

Resolution
  - Allow the robot to cool. It resumes automatically when in range.
  - Clear ventilation obstructions
  - If ambient exceeds 40 C, this is a facility issue. Zones near loading doors
    in summer are the usual offenders.
  - High cell voltage deviation with elevated cycle count means battery
    replacement. Follow the battery replacement procedure exactly, including the
    cycle-count transfer utility — replacing the battery management board
    without it resets cumulative cycle count and destroys the replacement
    history (known issue FW-1163).

E-611  —  LOCALIZATION CONFIDENCE LOST
=============================================================================

Meaning
  The robot cannot determine its position with sufficient confidence. It has
  performed a controlled stop and holds position. This is fail-static behavior
  working as designed.

Common causes
  1. Facility layout changed without a map update — the most common cause by a
     wide margin
  2. Highly repetitive rack geometry defeating scan matching
  3. Large moving obstructions occluding reference features
  4. Robot physically moved while powered off
  5. Corrupted or wrong facility map loaded

Diagnostics
  - Compare the facility map version against recent physical changes. Ask the
    customer directly whether anything moved. Racking changes, seasonal displays,
    and temporary staging areas are frequent culprits.
  - helixctl map version
  - Check whether E-611 clusters geographically. Clustering means a map problem;
    scattered events mean a robot problem.

Resolution
  - Re-localize from the Operator Console by specifying the robot's actual
    position
  - If the layout has genuinely changed, the facility map must be updated. Note
    that map editing is done in a desktop tool and imported as a binary blob
    with no diff and no rollback, so map updates require care. Map lifecycle
    management is on the 2025 roadmap.
  - Repetitive rack geometry was addressed in firmware 3.9.0, which fixed
    localization drift at Brightline Apparel Reno specifically. Their rack layout
    is unusually uniform and defeated the previous scan-matching heuristic.
  - Cold-start localization takes 9 seconds on firmware 3.9.0+ and 22 seconds on
    earlier firmware. Hardware revision A units in facilities above 60 percent
    rack occupancy can exceed 30 seconds (known issue FW-1171).

E-704  —  DRIVE MOTOR OVERCURRENT
=============================================================================

Meaning
  A drive motor drew current beyond its limit. The robot has stopped.

Common causes
  1. Physical obstruction jamming a wheel
  2. Debris wrapped around a wheel or axle — shrink wrap and strapping are the
     classic offenders
  3. Attempting to traverse a gradient or obstacle beyond capability
  4. Castor wheel seized
  5. Motor or drive board fault

Diagnostics
  - Visual inspection with the fleet paused and zone locked out
  - helixctl robot detail <ROBOT_ID> --telemetry, review motor current history
  - Note: motor current reporting scale factor was incorrect on units
    manufactured after 2024-06 with the revised drive board, on firmware earlier
    than 3.9.0. Verify firmware version before concluding a hardware fault from
    current readings.

Resolution
  - Remove obstruction or debris, acknowledge, resume
  - Check castor wheels for free rotation
  - Persistent E-704 with no visible obstruction means drive train or drive
    board fault. Take out of service and dispatch a field technician.


=============================================================================
HX-450 STACKER SPECIFIC CODES
=============================================================================

E-820  Pallet pocket detection failure
  Damaged stringers, missing blocks, shrink-wrap overhang, or poor pallet
  quality. Detection tolerates +/- 40 mm placement error and +/- 6 degrees
  rotation; beyond that it will refuse the pick. Sites running a mixed or
  poor-quality pallet pool see materially higher rates. Raise pallet quality
  during site assessment, not after deployment. The fork camera upgrade helps in
  low-contrast and low-light conditions.

E-822  Load stability fault
  Asymmetric or shifting load detected by fork load sensors and IMU. Inspect
  load distribution. Repeated events from one customer location usually mean a
  palletizing practice problem upstream.

E-826  Lift interlock violation
  The mast cannot rise while the robot moves above 0.3 m/s, and the robot cannot
  exceed 0.3 m/s with the mast extended. Enforced bidirectionally in firmware.
  This code generally indicates a Fleet OS planning issue or an attempted manual
  override. Escalate to Level 3; it should not occur in normal operation.

E-831  Overhead clearance obstruction
  Mast depth camera detected an obstruction above the lift path. Check for
  overhead conduit, sprinkler heads, lighting, or improperly stored inventory.
  Frequently a facility survey gap rather than a robot problem.

E-838  Hydraulic pressure out of range
  Lift hydraulic system fault. Take out of service and dispatch a field
  technician. Do not attempt customer-side remediation on the hydraulic system.


=============================================================================
CONDITIONS WITH NO FAULT CODE
=============================================================================

Some of the worst problems produce no fault code at all. These are the ones to
watch for, because every instinct trained on fault codes will miss them.

Robots healthy but idle, work queued
  Dispatch stall. Fleet Recovery Runbook section 3.

Robots accumulating at chargers, all reporting healthy, fleet availability
declining
  Charge dock reservation deadlock. Fleet Recovery Runbook section 4. Check
  Fleet OS and firmware versions first — the combination of Fleet OS 4.2.0 or
  4.2.1 with HX-200 firmware 3.8.1 is the INC-2024-017 exposure. This condition
  produced no fault codes, no alerts, and 41 minutes of undetected degradation
  before a customer telephoned us.

Throughput declining with no faults and no dock queue
  Check for facility changes, Wi-Fi degradation, or task mix changes. Compare
  against the site's throughput baseline.

Individual robot slower than fleet average, no faults
  Check battery cycle count and cell voltage deviation. A degrading battery
  reduces effective duty cycle before it produces any fault.

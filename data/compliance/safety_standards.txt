1. SCOPE AND AUTHORITY

Helix Robotics builds machines that move loads of up to 450 kg through buildings
where people work, continuously, without physical separation. Everything in this
document follows from that.

The Safety and Compliance function holds absolute veto over any change affecting
motion safety, on any product, at any point in the development cycle. This
authority is not advisory and does not route through engineering management. It
was established at company founding and has been exercised eleven times since
2021.

Changes requiring Safety and Compliance sign-off:

  - Any firmware change touching motion control, emergency stop handling,
    obstacle detection, speed envelope enforcement, or load stability monitoring
  - Any hardware change to the safety scanner chain, emergency stop hardware, or
    drive train
  - Any change to the interface between Fleet OS and firmware that could affect
    what commands firmware accepts
  - Any change to certified configuration defaults
  - Any facility map feature affecting zone classification

3. THE SAFETY ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE

The single most important statement in this document:

  All safety functions are implemented in robot firmware and in safety-rated
  hardware. Helix Fleet OS has no authority over any of them.

Fleet OS tells a robot where to go. Firmware decides how to get there and
retains absolute authority to refuse or abort any command that would violate a
safety constraint. Fleet OS cannot override an emergency stop, disable obstacle
detection, or command motion outside the certified speed envelope.

This is a certification requirement under ISO 3691-4 and it is not configurable,
not negotiable, and not bypassable — including for testing, including for
demonstrations, and including at customer request. There is no debug flag. There
is no service mode that relaxes it. Engineers who ask for one are told no.

The practical value of this separation was demonstrated during incident
INC-2024-017 in September 2024. A Fleet OS scheduling defect deadlocked 61 robots
at Cardinal Foods for nearly seven hours. The safety consequence was zero. No
robot moved unsafely, no near-miss was reported, no injury occurred, and no
property was damaged. The robots sat still, which is exactly what the
architecture is designed to make them do when the fleet layer is confused.

A fleet software defect should be an availability problem, never a safety
problem. That incident is our best evidence that the separation holds under real
failure conditions, and I have cited it in the 2024 surveillance audit.


4. CERTIFIED SAFETY FUNCTIONS

Common to both products:

  4.1  Protective field monitoring. Dual safety-rated 2D LiDAR scanners, front
       and rear, generating a protective field around the robot. Field violation
       triggers an immediate controlled stop. Always active, cannot be disabled.

  4.2  Emergency stop. Physical buttons on all four chassis faces, plus one at
       mast height on the HX-450. Wireless facility-wide stop via operator
       pendant. E-stop is a hardware-level function; software participates in
       reporting it, never in deciding it.

  4.3  Speed envelope enforcement. Maximum speeds are enforced in firmware
       against the certified envelope. HX-200: 2.0 m/s unladen, 1.6 m/s at rated
       load, 1.2 m/s in mixed-traffic zones. HX-450: 1.5 m/s unladen, 1.1 m/s at
       rated load, 0.9 m/s mixed-traffic, and 0.3 m/s with the mast extended.

  4.4  Dynamic speed envelope. Automatic speed reduction in zones flagged
       mixed-traffic in the facility map. Introduced in HX-200 firmware 3.7.0
       and included in the 2024 CE Declaration revision.

  4.5  Fail-static behavior. Loss of Fleet OS connectivity, loss of localization
       confidence, or any unrecoverable fault produces a controlled stop and
       hold position. Robots never continue under uncertainty.

HX-200 specific:

  4.6  Payload shift detection (E-118). IMU signature analysis halts the robot
       when a load moves in transit. Introduced in firmware 3.8.0 following
       three tote spill events at Cardinal Foods in Q2 2024. Assessed as a
       safety enhancement; no re-certification required, notified to the
       certification body at the 2024 surveillance.

HX-450 specific:

  4.7  Load stability monitoring (E-822). Fork load sensors plus IMU detect
       asymmetric or shifting loads.

  4.8  Lift interlock (E-826). The mast cannot rise while the robot exceeds
       0.3 m/s, and the robot cannot exceed 0.3 m/s with the mast extended.
       Enforced bidirectionally in firmware. This limit derives from the
       stability envelope calculated for rated load at maximum lift height. It
       cannot be raised by configuration, by Fleet OS, or by service tooling.
       Customers ask regularly. The answer is permanently no.

  4.9  Overhead clearance check (E-831). Mast depth camera verifies clearance
       before lift.

  4.10 Fault behavior with extended mast. On an unrecoverable fault with the
       mast extended, the robot holds the load at its current height rather than
       lowering it. Lowering under fault conditions was assessed as the more
       dangerous option and this behavior is part of the certified
       configuration.


5. CONFIGURATION BOUNDARIES

The following void certification if altered, and no Helix employee may assist a
customer in doing so:

  - Disabling or bypassing the safety scanner chain
  - Disabling the dynamic speed envelope
  - Raising any speed limit above the certified envelope
  - Removing or disabling emergency stop hardware
  - Operating outside the certified environment: the HX-200 is IP54 and
    certified for indoor use only. Outdoor and yard operation is not a
    configuration option and is not on any roadmap.
  - Operating above rated payload. The HX-200 derated mode (240 kg at 1.0 m/s)
    is part of the certified configuration; anything beyond it is not.
  - Modifying facility map zone classifications to remove mixed-traffic flags
    from areas where people are present


6. INCIDENT REPORTING OBLIGATIONS

Any event meeting the following must be reported to Safety and Compliance within
24 hours, regardless of severity assessment by support or engineering:

  - Any contact between a robot and a person, however minor
  - Any near-miss reported by customer or Helix staff
  - Any unexpected robot motion
  - Any failure of a safety function to operate as designed
  - Any e-stop that could not be cleared after physical reset, which indicates a
    fault in the e-stop chain
  - Any property damage caused by a robot

Reportable to the certification body: any failure of a certified safety function.
Reportable to regulators, jurisdiction-dependent: any injury.

Recorded for completeness: INC-2024-017 was not reportable under any of these
criteria. It was a fleet availability failure with no safety dimension. I make
this point because during the initial hours of that incident there was internal
uncertainty about whether it was safety-relevant, and the answer was determinable
immediately from the architecture — Fleet OS cannot cause unsafe motion. Knowing
that quickly matters during an incident, and it is why the safety architecture
principle in section 3 is the first thing every engineer learns.


7. TRAINING REQUIREMENTS

  - All engineering staff without exception, including those who will never
    touch firmware: two-hour safety orientation in week one. Required for Austin
    test bay access.
  - Field technicians: full certification programme, covering floor entry
    protocol, lockout procedure, and safety function verification. Renewed
    annually. Covered in the Field Technician Training Manual.
  - Customer facility operators: operator training delivered at deployment,
    covering e-stop use, floor entry protocol, and fault acknowledgement.
  - Anyone entering a robot operating area at any Helix or customer facility:
    floor entry protocol briefing, no exceptions, including executives and
    visitors.


8. OPEN COMPLIANCE ITEMS

  - HX-200 hardware revision D (H2 2025) includes a single-vendor LiDAR strategy
    that touches the safety scanner chain and will require IEC 61508 SIL 2
    re-assessment. I have been engaged from Q1 2025 rather than at design
    freeze, which is the correct sequencing and a change from how revision C was
    handled. Re-assessment timelines are set by the certification body and are
    the critical path.
  - Fleet OS 5.0 unifies the Traffic Manager and Charge Scheduler reservation
    systems. This is fleet-layer work with no direct safety function impact, but
    it is a large refactor adjacent to motion planning and I have asked to review
    the interface boundary specifically.
  - EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 replaces the Machinery Directive from
    January 2027. Gap assessment scheduled Q2 2025. Early reading suggests
    limited impact on our certified configuration, but the substantial changes
    around software and cybersecurity require proper analysis rather than
    optimism.
  - Two aisles at Trellis Home Goods Columbus operate below 200 lux, degrading
    Helix Vision obstacle classification. This is not a certified safety function
    — the safety scanner chain is unaffected by lighting — but it produces
    increased conservative stops and I have asked that the site requirement for
    illumination be stated explicitly in future site assessments rather than
    assumed.

1. WHAT DATA HELIX HANDLES

Helix operates robots inside customer facilities. The data we handle divides into
three categories with materially different sensitivity.

OPERATIONAL TELEMETRY
  Robot pose, velocity, battery state, motor currents, fault flags, task
  identifiers, and dock state. Emitted at 4 Hz per robot, retained locally for
  90 days, and exported to Helix Cloud at a downsampled 0.2 Hz for support and
  analytics.

  This is machine data. It contains no personal information by design, and the
  telemetry schema is reviewed at each release to keep it that way.

CUSTOMER BUSINESS DATA
  Task instructions from the customer WMS, which necessarily reveal what is
  moving where and in what volume. This is commercially sensitive to the
  customer even though it is not personal data.

  Task content is processed in the Site Adapter and Dispatcher. It is retained in
  the local task history. It is NOT exported to Helix Cloud. Only aggregate
  counts leave the facility.

PERSONAL DATA
  Limited and deliberately so:
    - Operator Console user accounts: name, work email, role, authentication
      credentials, and access log entries
    - Support ticket contents, which frequently include customer staff names and
      contact details
    - Helix employee data, handled under separate People Ops policy

  Helix Vision detects and classifies the presence of a person as an obstacle
  class. It does not perform identification, does not retain images, and does not
  transmit images. Classification output is a category label, not a picture.
  This is an architectural constraint, not a configuration setting, and it exists
  specifically so that deploying our robots does not create a workplace
  surveillance capability. Customer works councils in Germany and the
  Netherlands have asked about this directly; the answer above is complete and
  accurate and may be given in full.


2. DATA LOCATION AND RESIDENCY

Fleet OS runs on-premise in the customer facility. All operational processing,
all task data, and all live floor state remain on customer premises.

Telemetry export to Helix Cloud currently lands in US regions. This is the
principal open issue in this policy.

  - North American customers: no residency concern raised to date.
  - EMEA customers: telemetry export operates under a data processing agreement
    with standard contractual clauses. Voss Pharma has accepted this for their
    Basel site and has stated clearly that EU data residency is a requirement for
    any expansion into Frankfurt or Dublin.
  - EU data residency for telemetry export is on the 2025 roadmap under EMEA
    deployment tooling, scheduled H2 2025.

Customers may disable telemetry export entirely. Doing so degrades our support
capability substantially — telemetry retention was what made root cause analysis
of INC-2024-017 conclusive rather than speculative, since it let us reconstruct
the full eleven-day accumulation of stuck robots. Customers should understand
that tradeoff, and it should be presented honestly rather than as a reason not to
offer the option.

Fleet OS 4.2.3 added the ability to pause and resume telemetry export without
restarting the Telemetry Pipeline. This exists because Voss Pharma's change
control process requires the ability to suspend outbound data flow during audit
windows.


3. RETENTION

  Local telemetry (customer premises)          90 days, rolling
  Exported telemetry (Helix Cloud)             13 months
  Task history (customer premises)             24 months, customer-configurable
  Operator Console audit log                   24 months minimum
  Support tickets and attachments              5 years
  Diagnostic captures                          90 days unless attached to an
                                               open incident
  Incident records and postmortems             Indefinite

Diagnostic captures deserve particular attention. A full capture
(helixctl diag capture --full) includes recent task history and can therefore
contain customer business data. Captures must be stored in the support system
against the relevant ticket, never on personal machines, never in shared drives,
and never attached to email. Field technicians and support engineers are trained
on this specifically.


4. ACCESS CONTROL — INTERNAL

  - Read access to production telemetry across all customer sites is available to
    engineering staff through the support tooling. This is broad by design; the
    ability to investigate a problem at any site is operationally necessary.
  - Write access to anything customer-facing requires separate approval and is
    audited.
  - Access to customer task history requires a justification recorded against a
    ticket or incident.
  - Access to Operator Console user account data is restricted to Support and
    SRE.

All access to customer environments is logged. Logs are reviewed quarterly by
Safety and Compliance. Two reviews have been conducted since this policy took
effect, both with no findings.


5. THE OPERATOR CONSOLE AUTHENTICATION GAP

Stated plainly because it is the largest known control weakness in the product.

The Operator Console does not support single sign-on. Authentication uses local
accounts managed within Fleet OS. This means:

  - No enforcement of the customer's password policy
  - No enforcement of the customer's MFA requirement
  - No centralized deprovisioning. When a customer employee leaves, their Fleet
    OS account must be disabled manually and separately from their normal
    offboarding.
  - Audit log exists but is not tamper-evident

For customers with formal identity governance requirements this is a genuine
compliance gap, not a feature preference. Voss Pharma's IT Security and
Compliance Lead has stated in writing that it is non-compliant with their
identity governance standard, and Basel operates today under a documented
compensating control — quarterly manual account reconciliation performed jointly
by customer operations and customer security — that is an exception with an
expiry date.

SAML and OIDC single sign-on, role-based access control refinement, and a
tamper-evident audit log are roadmap item 3.1, scheduled H1 2025. Approximately
4.4 million USD of pipeline is gated on it.

Until it ships, the guidance for customers with identity governance requirements
is: document a compensating control, define its review cadence, and give it an
expiry. Do not represent the current state as adequate.


6. CUSTOMER DATA RIGHTS

Customers may at any time:

  - Request an export of all data Helix holds relating to their deployment
  - Request deletion of exported telemetry, subject to the retention required for
    open incidents
  - Disable telemetry export entirely
  - Pause telemetry export for a defined window
  - Audit Helix's handling of their data. Voss Pharma exercises this annually;
    the 2024 audit completed in October with two findings, both closed.

Requests route through the Technical Account Manager for Enterprise customers and
through Support otherwise. Response commitment is 30 days, and we have met it on
every request to date.


7. SUB-PROCESSORS

Helix Cloud runs on a single major infrastructure provider. Sub-processors used
in the handling of customer telemetry or support data are listed in the data
processing addendum and customers are notified 30 days before any change.

No customer telemetry or business data is used to train machine learning models
without explicit written customer agreement. Two customers have agreed to
participate in the perception training programme, under agreements that cover
Helix Vision obstacle classification data only. No task content, no personal
data, and no facility layout data is included.


8. BREACH RESPONSE

  - Suspected breach reported to Safety and Compliance immediately, at any hour.
  - Initial assessment within 4 hours.
  - Affected customers notified within 72 hours of confirmation, or sooner where
    contractually or legally required.
  - Written incident report within 5 business days, following the same structure
    as our safety and availability postmortems: the actual mechanism, the actual
    process gap, named owners with dates on remediation.

No customer data breach has occurred to date. One security defect has been
remediated: a TLS certificate validation weakness in the telemetry upload path,
reported through the Helix responsible disclosure process and patched in HX-200
firmware 3.6.4 on 2024-11-19. There was no evidence of exploitation, and affected
customers were notified.


9. RESPONSIBLE DISCLOSURE

Helix operates a responsible disclosure programme. Reports are acknowledged
within 2 business days and triaged within 5. We do not pursue legal action
against good-faith researchers. Contact details are published on the Helix
website and in customer documentation.

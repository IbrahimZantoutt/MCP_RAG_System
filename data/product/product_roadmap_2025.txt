STRATEGIC CONTEXT

Helix enters 2025 with 412 employees, 33.2 million USD in ARR, and roughly 1,510
robots deployed across 38 customer sites. We are not yet profitable; Q3 2024
closed at a net loss of 4.1 million USD with 61 million USD cash on hand. The
board's expectation is that we reach operating breakeven during 2026, which means
2025 is the year we convert engineering investment into gross margin rather than
into new product surface area.

Three things constrain the 2025 plan.

First, INC-2024-017 cost us 148,000 USD in SLA credits and a substantial amount
of trust with our largest customer. The remediation is complete and Cardinal
Foods renewed, but the incident exposed structural debt — two subsystems solving
resource reservation differently, no cross-version testing, alerting that only
looked at individual robots. We are spending real 2025 capacity paying that down
rather than shipping features, and that is the correct call.

Second, gross margin at 51 percent is below where a hardware-plus-software
business needs to be. The software attach rate is good; the hardware bill of
materials is not. Cost reduction on the HX-200 is a first-class roadmap item.

Third, we are losing deals in two identifiable places: facilities with high-bay
racking that the HX-450 cannot reach, and regulated customers who require SSO
and stronger audit controls. Voss Pharma has made SSO an explicit blocker on
expansion beyond Basel.


THEME 1 — RELIABILITY AND ARCHITECTURAL DEBT

The largest single allocation in the 2025 plan, at roughly 30 percent of
engineering capacity in H1.

1.1  Fleet OS 5.0 — unified reservation system                      H1 2025
     Merge the Traffic Manager and Charge Scheduler reservation implementations
     onto a single leased-resource model. This is postmortem action item AI-9
     from INC-2024-017, deferred from the hotfix as tracked architectural debt.
     The 4.2.2 leases closed the operational risk; this closes the structural
     one. Owner: Jae-won Park.

1.2  Facility map lifecycle management                              H1 2025
     Maps are currently edited in a desktop tool and imported as an opaque
     binary blob with no diff, no review, and no rollback. A bad map import is
     currently a site-down event with no undo. Introduce versioned map storage,
     visual diff, review workflow, and one-click rollback. Owner: Grace Chen
     with Fleet Software.

1.3  Site Adapter sandboxing                                        H2 2025
     Adapter plugins run in-process and unsandboxed. A misbehaving adapter can
     consume unbounded memory in the Fleet OS process and take down the facility.
     Move adapters to a supervised out-of-process model with resource limits.

1.4  Predictive battery health                                      H2 2025
     Firmware 3.9.0 added per-cell voltage deviation and cumulative cycle count
     to telemetry. Build the model that turns that into scheduled replacement
     before failure. Requested by Field Operations; batteries are currently
     replaced reactively, which means an unplanned robot outage every time.

1.5  Expanded cross-version CI matrix                               H1 2025
     The matrix established in November 2024 covers Fleet OS against HX-200
     firmware. Extend to HX-450 2.x firmware, dock firmware, and Helix Vision
     module firmware. Every independently versioned artifact in the system
     should be in the matrix. Owner: Deepa Nair.


THEME 2 — MARGIN

Roughly 20 percent of engineering capacity, concentrated in hardware.

2.1  HX-200 hardware revision D — cost reduction                    H2 2025
     Target: 14 percent bill-of-materials reduction without changing the
     certified safety envelope. Principal levers are drive board integration,
     a single-vendor LiDAR strategy replacing the current dual-source approach,
     and chassis part-count reduction. Any change touching the safety scanner
     chain requires re-assessment against IEC 61508 SIL 2, which is a long pole;
     Nikolai Petrov is engaged from the start rather than at the end.

2.2  Helix Vision cost-down and attach rate                         H1 2025
     Vision attaches on only 31 percent of units. It is high margin and it
     materially improves obstacle handling. Reduce module cost and simplify the
     upsell by making the base capability visible in the Operator Console for
     non-Vision fleets, so customers can see what they are missing.

2.3  Remote diagnostics and reduced truck rolls                     H2 2025
     Field Operations dispatches a technician for a meaningful fraction of
     issues that are diagnosable remotely. Each avoided truck roll is direct
     margin. Build the remote diagnostic capture and guided-resolution flow into
     the support tooling.


THEME 3 — MARKET EXPANSION

Roughly 35 percent of engineering capacity.

3.1  Operator Console SSO and audit controls                        H1 2025
     SAML and OIDC single sign-on, role-based access control refinement, and a
     complete tamper-evident audit log. This is an explicit expansion blocker at
     Voss Pharma and appears in the objection log on most regulated-industry
     deals. Highest-confidence revenue unlock on the roadmap.

3.2  HX-700 high-bay concept — feasibility only                     H1 2025
     The HX-450 reaches 1,800 mm on the base mast and 2,400 mm extended. Neither
     reaches high-bay racking, and we are systematically absent from that
     segment. This is a feasibility study and a business case in H1, not a
     product commitment. A decision gate in July 2025 determines whether it
     enters development. Nobody should represent this to a customer as planned.

3.3  Mixed-fleet planning efficiency                                H1 2025
     Fleet OS 4.2 supports mixed HX-200 and HX-450 fleets, but traffic planning
     for mixed fleets is measurably worse than for homogeneous fleets. As more
     sites run both models this becomes a throughput tax on exactly our largest
     accounts.

3.4  EMEA deployment tooling                                        H2 2025
     Rotterdam is running EMEA deployments on tooling built for North American
     sites. Multi-language Operator Console, EU data residency for telemetry
     export, and CE documentation generation.

3.5  Cold-chain HX-450 improvements                                 H2 2025
     The -25 C variant works but runtime drops to about 4.5 hours and charge
     time extends roughly 40 percent. Several early freezer proposals were
     sized without accounting for this and underdelivered. Improve low-
     temperature battery performance and fix the site sizing model.


THEME 4 — CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

Roughly 15 percent of engineering capacity.

4.1  Self-service site health dashboard                             H1 2025
     Customers currently ask their TAM for information that should be visible to
     them directly. Fleet availability, throughput against baseline, dock
     utilization, fault trends.

4.2  Guided troubleshooting in the Operator Console                 H2 2025
     Bring the Troubleshooting Guide's fault-code procedures directly into the
     console, contextual to the actual fault. Reduce the volume of tickets that
     are really documentation lookups.

4.3  Upgrade orchestration                                          H2 2025
     Fleet OS and firmware upgrades are currently coordinated manually between
     Field Operations and the customer. Given that INC-2024-017 required
     deploying two coordinated patches across 240 robots, and that partial
     firmware rollouts create exactly the kind of mixed-version exposure that
     caused it, this deserves real tooling.


EXPLICITLY NOT ON THE 2025 ROADMAP

Recorded so that the answer is consistent when these come up, which they will.

  - Outdoor or yard operations. The HX-200 is IP54 and the safety certification
    covers indoor mixed-traffic use only. This is not a firmware change; it is a
    different product.
  - Cloud-hosted Fleet OS control plane. Robot motion will not depend on
    internet connectivity. This is a deliberate architectural commitment, not an
    unbuilt feature.
  - Multi-tenant Fleet OS. Each facility gets an isolated instance. Customers
    with multiple DCs run multiple instances.
  - Automatic warm-standby failover. Manual failover is a safety decision, not a
    missing capability. Two controllers that both believe they own the floor is
    worse than a two-minute human cutover.
  - Raising the HX-450 mast-extended speed limit above 0.3 m/s. Asked for
    regularly. Permanently declined; it is tied to the stability envelope.
  - Third-party robot support in Fleet OS. Occasionally raised by prospects with
    existing mixed vendor fleets. Not in 2025.


DEPENDENCIES AND RISKS

  - Theme 2.1 (hardware cost reduction) depends on safety re-assessment
    timelines that are outside our control. If IEC 61508 re-assessment slips,
    revision D slips with it. Mitigation: engage Nikolai Petrov and the
    certification body in Q1 rather than Q3.
  - Theme 3.1 (SSO) is the single highest-confidence revenue item and should be
    protected from reallocation. If H1 capacity comes under pressure, 3.2
    (HX-700 feasibility) yields first.
  - Theme 1.1 (Fleet OS 5.0 reservation unification) is a large refactor of the
    two most safety-adjacent subsystems in the product. It carries genuine
    regression risk. It is also the correct fix for the structural cause of our
    worst incident. Deepa Nair's team is staffing dedicated test capacity
    against it from the start.
  - Hiring plan assumes 34 net engineering additions in 2025. Slower hiring
    compresses Theme 3 first.

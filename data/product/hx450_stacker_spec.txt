1. PRODUCT SUMMARY

The HX-450 Stacker is Helix Robotics' pallet-handling autonomous mobile robot.
Where the HX-200 Courier moves totes and cases, the Stacker moves full pallets,
lifts them to racking height, and handles the heavier end of intralogistics work.

The Stacker is a newer and lower-volume product than the Courier. First shipped
in March 2023, it represents 22 percent of units shipped and roughly 34 percent
of hardware revenue, reflecting its substantially higher unit price. Deployed
fleet as of Q4 2024 is approximately 330 units across 14 customer sites.

The Stacker runs the 2.x firmware line, versioned independently from the
Courier's 3.x line. This is a frequent source of confusion for new staff: HX-200
firmware 3.8.1 and HX-450 firmware 2.x are unrelated releases on unrelated
lines. The charge dock deadlock of INC-2024-017 affected HX-200 units running
firmware 3.8.1 only. HX-450 units were not affected, because the 2.x firmware
line never adopted the SoC hysteresis band that caused the interaction.

  Lift motor                      1.5 kW

  Lift speed, unladen             0.30 m/s
  Maximum speed, unladen          1.5 m/s
  Maximum speed, rated load       1.1 m/s
  Maximum speed, mast extended    0.3 m/s (firmware enforced, non-negotiable)
  Maximum speed, mixed-traffic    0.9 m/s
  Emergency stop distance         Under 500 mm at 1.1 m/s with rated load
  Gradient capability             2 percent sustained, laden
  Fork positioning accuracy       +/- 8 mm horizontal, +/- 5 mm vertical

The mast-extended speed limit of 0.3 m/s is a hard firmware constraint tied to
the stability envelope calculated for the rated load at maximum lift height. It
cannot be raised by configuration, by Fleet OS, or by service tooling. Several
customers have asked; the answer is permanently no.


4. POWER AND CHARGING

  Battery chemistry               LiFePO4
  Capacity                        11.2 kWh
  Nominal voltage                 48 V
  Runtime, typical duty cycle     7.0 hours
  Runtime, continuous heavy duty  4.5 hours
  Charge time, 20 to 90 percent   110 minutes
  Charge time, 20 to 100 percent  185 minutes
  Charging method                 Automatic docking, conductive contacts
  Dock ratio, recommended         One dock per three robots
  Rated cycle life                4,000 cycles to 80 percent capacity

The recommended dock ratio is higher than the Courier's (one per three rather
than one per four) because the Stacker's longer charge time and shorter runtime
compress the available duty window. Under-provisioning docks is the single most
common site design error on Stacker deployments and produces chronic throughput
shortfall that customers frequently misattribute to robot performance.

As with the Courier, charging is orchestrated by the Fleet OS Charge Scheduler.
The Stacker's 2.x firmware reports state of charge as an integer percentage and
has never used a hysteresis band, which is why the HX-450 fleet was unaffected
by INC-2024-017. The dock reservation lease fix shipped in Fleet OS 4.2.2
nonetheless applies to Stacker fleets and is recommended for all sites.


5. SENSING AND NAVIGATION

  Primary navigation              2D LiDAR, 360 degree, 25 m range, 15 Hz
  Safety scanners                 Dual safety-rated 2D LiDAR, front and rear,
                                  IEC 61508 SIL 2
  Mast-mounted sensing            Downward-facing depth camera for fork
                                  alignment and pallet pocket detection
  Load presence                   Fork-mounted load sensors, four points
  Secondary sensing               Helix Vision stereo module (optional)
  Odometry                        Wheel encoders plus 6-axis IMU
  Localization method             Scan matching against facility map, fused
                                  with odometry
  Pallet detection                Depth camera pocket detection, tolerant of
                                  +/- 40 mm placement error and +/- 6 degrees
                                  rotation

Pallet condition matters more than most customers expect. Damaged stringers,
missing blocks, and heavily shrink-wrapped overhang all degrade pocket detection.
Sites running a mixed or poor-quality pallet pool see materially higher pick
failure rates. This should be raised during site assessment, not discovered
after deployment.


6. SAFETY ARCHITECTURE

The Stacker carries all HX-200 safety functions plus additional constraints
specific to lifting:

  - Dual safety-rated LiDAR protective fields, firmware authority, always active
  - Physical emergency stop on all four chassis faces plus one at mast height
  - Wireless facility-wide emergency stop via operator pendant
  - Speed envelope enforcement including the mast-extended limit
  - Load stability monitoring. Fork load sensors plus IMU detect load shift or
    asymmetric loading and halt the robot.
  - Lift interlock. The mast cannot be raised while the robot is in motion above
    0.3 m/s, and the robot cannot accelerate above 0.3 m/s while the mast is
    extended. Enforced in firmware, bidirectionally.
  - Overhead clearance check before lift, using the mast depth camera
  - Fail-static behavior. On any unrecoverable fault the robot performs a
    controlled stop, holds position, and if the mast is extended it holds the
    load at its current height rather than lowering. Lowering under fault
    conditions was judged the more dangerous option.

Certification: ISO 3691-4, ANSI/RIA R15.08, IEC 61508 SIL 2 safety scanner
chain, UL 3100, CE marking. The Stacker required a separate certification
assessment from the Courier due to the lifting function; it is not covered by
the Courier's certificate.

9. KNOWN LIMITATIONS

  - Maximum lift height of 1,800 mm on the base mast limits the Stacker to
    ground-level and first-rack-level work in most facilities. The extended mast
    reaches 2,400 mm. Neither reaches high-bay racking, and we do not currently
    compete in that segment.
  - Sensitive to pallet quality. See section 5.
  - Higher dock ratio requirement than the Courier. Under-provisioning is the
    most common site design error.
  - Cannot handle slip sheets, half pallets under 600 mm, or non-standard
    pallet geometries without a custom fork configuration.
  - Aisle width requirements are substantially higher than the Courier's. Some
    facilities that can accept a Courier fleet cannot accept Stackers without
    rack reconfiguration.
  - Mixed HX-200 and HX-450 fleets share floor space and are managed by a single
    Fleet OS instance, but traffic planning for mixed fleets is less efficient
    than for homogeneous fleets. Improved mixed-fleet planning is on the 2025
    roadmap.
  - Cold-chain variant is rated to -25 C but battery runtime degrades to
    approximately 4.5 hours typical at that temperature, and charge time extends
    by roughly 40 percent. Site designs for freezer applications must account for
    this; several early proposals did not.

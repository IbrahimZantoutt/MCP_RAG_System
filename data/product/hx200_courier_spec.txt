1. PRODUCT SUMMARY

The HX-200 Courier is Helix Robotics' tote and case-handling autonomous mobile
robot. It is the company's highest-volume product, representing 78 percent of
units shipped and the substantial majority of hardware revenue. It moves totes,
cases, and light containers between pick faces, packing stations, staging areas,
and outbound docks in distribution and fulfillment environments.

The Courier is designed for facilities where robots and humans share floor space
continuously. It is certified for mixed-traffic operation under ISO 3691-4 and
carries the safety architecture required for that classification.

First shipped: June 2021. Current hardware revision C, shipping since June 2024.
Deployed fleet as of Q4 2024: approximately 1,180 units across 31 customer sites.
Largest single deployment: Cardinal Foods Memphis distribution center, 240 units.

3. DRIVE AND MOTION

  Drive configuration             Differential drive, two powered wheels,
                                  four passive castors
  Motors                          2 x 400 W brushless DC, integrated encoders
  Maximum speed, unladen          2.0 m/s
  Maximum speed, rated load       1.6 m/s
  Maximum speed, mixed-traffic    1.2 m/s (enforced by firmware in zones
                                  flagged mixed-traffic in the facility map)
  Acceleration                    0.8 m/s^2 nominal
  Emergency stop distance         Under 350 mm at 1.2 m/s with rated load
  Gradient capability             3 percent sustained, 5 percent transient
  Positioning accuracy            +/- 10 mm at pick face, +/- 2 degrees heading

Speed envelopes are enforced in firmware and cannot be overridden by Fleet OS.
The dynamic speed envelope feature, which automatically reduces maximum speed in
mixed-traffic zones, was introduced in firmware 3.7.0 and is part of the
ISO 3691-4 certified configuration. Disabling it voids the certification.


4. POWER AND CHARGING

  Battery chemistry               LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate)
  Capacity                        6.4 kWh
  Nominal voltage                 48 V
  Runtime, typical duty cycle     8.5 hours
  Runtime, continuous heavy duty  6.0 hours
  Charge time, 20 to 90 percent   75 minutes
  Charge time, 20 to 100 percent  135 minutes
  Charging method                 Automatic docking, conductive contacts
  Dock ratio, recommended         One dock per four robots
  Rated cycle life                4,000 cycles to 80 percent capacity
  Expected battery service life   Approximately 5 years at typical duty

LiFePO4 was selected over higher-density chemistries specifically for thermal
safety and cycle life. In a facility containing flammable inventory and
continuous human presence, thermal runaway risk dominates the energy density
tradeoff. This decision is revisited annually and has been reaffirmed each time.

Charging behavior is orchestrated by the Fleet OS Charge Scheduler, not by the
robot. The robot reports state of charge; Fleet OS decides when it charges and
which dock it uses. The interface between these — how state of charge is
reported and how thresholds are compared — is the site of incident INC-2024-017.
Any change to SoC reporting semantics in firmware is now classified as a
cross-subsystem interface change and requires review under the two-approver rule
plus a cross-version CI matrix pass.

Minimum firmware for correct charge threshold behavior: 3.8.2.
Minimum Fleet OS for correct dock reservation handling: 4.2.2.
Both are required. See the postmortem for INC-2024-017.


5. SENSING AND NAVIGATION

  Primary navigation              2D LiDAR, 360 degree, 25 m range, 15 Hz
  Safety scanner                  Dual safety-rated 2D LiDAR, front and rear,
                                  certified to IEC 61508 SIL 2
  Secondary sensing               Helix Vision stereo module (optional add-on)
  Odometry                        Wheel encoders plus 6-axis IMU
  Localization method             Scan matching against a facility map, fused
                                  with odometry
  Cold-start localization         9 seconds typical (firmware 3.9.0 and later);
                                  22 seconds on earlier firmware
  Obstacle detection              Safety scanners, always active, firmware
                                  authority. Classification requires Helix
                                  Vision.
  Fixed obstacle envelope         400 mm (without Helix Vision)
  Adaptive obstacle envelope      Modulated by obstacle class (with Helix Vision
                                  and firmware 3.9.0 or later)

Localization depends on facility map quality. Facilities with highly repetitive
rack geometry can defeat scan matching; this affected Brightline Apparel Reno and
was addressed in firmware 3.9.0. Facilities below 200 lux in the aisles degrade
Helix Vision classification, which affects two aisles at Trellis Home Goods
Columbus. Lighting is a facility-side requirement documented in the site
requirements guide.


6. SAFETY ARCHITECTURE

The HX-200 is certified for operation in shared human-robot workspaces. Its
safety functions are implemented entirely in firmware and in safety-rated
hardware. Fleet OS has no authority over any of them.

  - Dual safety-rated LiDAR scanners providing a protective field around the
    robot. Field violation triggers immediate controlled stop.
  - Physical emergency stop button on all four faces of the chassis.
  - Wireless emergency stop, facility-wide, via the operator pendant.
  - Speed envelope enforcement, including automatic reduction in mixed-traffic
    zones.
  - Payload shift detection (E-118), using IMU signature analysis to halt the
    robot when a load moves during transit. Added in firmware 3.8.0 following
    three tote spill events at Cardinal Foods in Q2 2024.
  - Fail-static behavior. Loss of Fleet OS connectivity, loss of localization
    confidence, or any unrecoverable fault results in a controlled stop and hold
    position. The robot never continues under uncertainty.

Certification: ISO 3691-4, ANSI/RIA R15.08, IEC 61508 SIL 2 for the safety
scanner chain, UL 3100, CE marking for EMEA deployment. Full details are
maintained in the Safety Standards and Certification document owned by Nikolai
Petrov.


7. FAULT CODES

The HX-200 reports faults on the telemetry channel. Full diagnostic procedures
are in the Support Troubleshooting Guide; the codes are listed here for
reference.

  E-115   Emergency stop engaged
  E-118   Payload shift detected
  E-207   LiDAR occlusion — sensor obstructed or excessively dirty
  E-330   WMS sync failure (Fleet OS side, surfaced on the robot)
  E-401   Charge reservation timeout
  E-502   Battery thermal cutoff
  E-611   Localization confidence lost
  E-704   Drive motor overcurrent

9. KNOWN LIMITATIONS

Recorded openly for internal planning and for honest conversations with
prospects. Sales staff may discuss these; they are not confidential.

  - Not rated for outdoor use or for yard operations. IP54 covers dust and
    splash, not weather.
  - Cannot handle pallets. Pallet handling requires the HX-450 Stacker.
  - Requires a prepared floor. Floor flatness must meet the Helix site
    requirements; expansion joints wider than 12 mm require ramping.
  - Requires facility Wi-Fi meeting the Helix RF site requirements. Coverage
    gaps produce task reassignment churn rather than unsafe behavior, but they
    are disruptive at high fleet density.
  - Hardware revision A units (under 8 percent of the deployed fleet) have
    slower cold-start localization in dense facilities and are being retired
    through normal replacement rather than recalled.
  - Maximum practical fleet size per Fleet OS instance is 400 units. Larger
    facilities require partitioning into multiple instances, which cannot share
    floor space.
  - No SSO support in the Operator Console. Voss Pharma has raised this as a
    compliance blocker for their Basel deployment; it is on the 2025 roadmap.

4.2.3  —  2024-10-21  —  MAINTENANCE
=============================================================================

Features

  - Bulk stale dock reservation clearing. The Operator Console gains a single
    action (Fleet > Charging > Clear Stale Reservations) and the CLI gains
    "helixctl dock reservations clear --stale". This exists because clearing 61
    reservations individually during INC-2024-017 consumed 2 hours 20 minutes of
    operator time. Postmortem action item AI-6.

  - Pick-face allocator reservations now carry expiry leases. Found during the
    audit of all resource reservations in Fleet OS for missing expiry semantics
    (postmortem action item AI-7). Two unleased reservation types were found
    beyond the charging docks; both are corrected here.

  - Telemetry export can now be paused and resumed without restarting the
    Telemetry Pipeline. Requested by Voss Pharma, whose change control process
    requires the ability to suspend outbound data flow during audits.

  - Fleet-level alerting rules gained configurable thresholds per site. The rules
    themselves shipped in 4.2.2; this release makes them tunable, since baseline
    throughput and acceptable dock queue depth vary substantially between a
    240-unit grocery DC and a 30-unit apparel facility.

Fixes

  - Fixed Dispatcher assigning tasks to robots that were eligible at evaluation
    time but had entered a charge cycle before assignment landed. Produced
    harmless but confusing task churn.
  - Fixed Operator Console fleet-status page timing out on deployments above 200
    robots. Affected Cardinal Foods Memphis only, being the only site of that
    size.
  - Corrected Site Adapter retry backoff, which was retrying too aggressively
    against WMS endpoints and occasionally contributing to the very timeouts it
    was retrying against.

4.2.2  —  2024-09-16  —  CRITICAL HOTFIX
=============================================================================

This release remediates incident INC-2024-017. It must be deployed together
with HX-200 firmware 3.8.2 or later. Neither patch alone is sufficient; they fix
different halves of the same interaction.

Any site running Fleet OS 4.2.0 or 4.2.1 with HX-200 firmware 3.8.1 is exposed
to the charge dock reservation deadlock and must upgrade both.

Fixes

  - Charging dock reservations now carry expiry leases, matching the semantics
    the Traffic Manager has always used for floor segment reservations. A dock
    reservation that shows no progress within its lease window is reclaimed
    automatically. This is the more important of the two fixes in this release:
    it converts an unrecoverable deadlock into a self-healing condition.
    Postmortem action item AI-1.

  - Charge Scheduler state-of-charge comparisons now use a tolerance band rather
    than strict inequality against the robot's reported value. The previous
    implementation assumed reported SoC was a precise monotonic quantity, an
    assumption that was undocumented and was silently invalidated when firmware
    3.8.1 introduced a 0.5 percent hysteresis band. Postmortem action item AI-2.

  - Fleet-level alerting added. Three new rule categories, each with a defined
    threshold and page:
      * robots available for dispatch, sustained decline
      * charging dock queue depth
      * fleet throughput deviation from site baseline
    Prior to this release, alerting covered only individual robot health. During
    INC-2024-017 every affected robot reported itself perfectly healthy while
    the fleet lost 71 percent of throughput, and no alert fired for 41 minutes —
    at which point a customer shift supervisor telephoned support. Postmortem
    action item AI-4.

Background

On 2024-09-14 at Cardinal Foods' Memphis distribution center, 61 of 240 HX-200
units entered a state where they held charging dock reservations that would
never be released. Fleet throughput degraded progressively over 6 hours 47
minutes, peaking at 71 percent loss. The customer missed two outbound trailer
departures. The event resulted in a 148,000 USD SLA credit under their
Enterprise agreement.

The mechanism: firmware 3.8.1 introduced a hysteresis band on reported state of
charge for display smoothing. A robot arriving at a dock near the 20 percent
charge threshold could latch a reported value in the 20.0 to 20.5 percent range
while physically charging normally to full. Fleet OS 4.2.1 compared that reported
value against the release target using a strict inequality, so the release
condition was never satisfied. Dock reservations had no expiry, so nothing
reclaimed the dock. The robot sat fully charged, holding a dock, reporting
healthy, forever.

Both firmware 3.8.1 and Fleet OS 4.2.1 were individually correct and individually
well tested. They had never been tested against each other, because no automated
cross-version test matrix existed. That matrix now exists and runs nightly
(postmortem action item AI-5, completed 2024-11-08).

Full detail is in the INC-2024-017 postmortem. The diagnostic and recovery
procedure is section 4 of the Fleet Recovery Runbook.

4.2.1  —  2024-08-27  —  MAINTENANCE  —  WITHDRAWN
=============================================================================

WITHDRAWN. Vulnerable to the INC-2024-017 charge dock reservation deadlock when
paired with HX-200 firmware 3.8.1. Upgrade to 4.2.2 or later.

Changes as originally released

  - Fixed Site Adapter connection pooling leak against SAP EWM endpoints.
  - Reduced Operator Console initial load time by approximately 40 percent.
  - Fixed a Traffic Manager path replanning inefficiency that caused unnecessary
    detours around recently vacated segments.
  - Corrected time zone handling in the task history export, which had been
    emitting UTC timestamps labelled as site-local.


=============================================================================
4.2.0  —  2024-08-12  —  GENERAL AVAILABILITY  —  WITHDRAWN
=============================================================================

WITHDRAWN. See 4.2.1 note above; the same exposure applies.

This was the major release of 2024 and introduced the following.

Features

  - New Charge Scheduler. Replaced the 4.1 round-robin dock assignment with a
    constrained optimization over projected task demand, robot state of charge,
    dock availability, and throughput commitments. In field trials this improved
    effective fleet availability by 6 to 9 percent at sites with tight dock
    ratios.

    It is worth recording plainly that the new Charge Scheduler was written by a
    different team, at a different time, from the Traffic Manager, and did not
    adopt the Traffic Manager's lease-based reservation model. That divergence
    is what made INC-2024-017 unrecoverable rather than merely annoying. Full
    unification of the two reservation systems is tracked for Fleet OS 5.0.

  - Mixed-fleet support. A single Fleet OS instance can now manage HX-200 and
    HX-450 units sharing floor space, with capability-aware task assignment.
    Planning efficiency for mixed fleets remains below that of homogeneous
    fleets; improvement is on the 2025 roadmap.

  - Facility map versioning. Maps now carry a version identifier and change
    history. Note that this is metadata only — maps are still edited in a
    desktop tool and imported as a binary blob, with no diff, no review, and no
    rollback. Proper map lifecycle management remains tracked architectural debt.

  - Traffic Manager rewrite for large fleets. Scales cleanly to 400 robots per
    instance, up from a practical ceiling of roughly 250 in 4.1.

  - Operator Console redesign, including live floor visualization at fleet scale
    and per-robot drill-down.

  - Warm standby support for Enterprise-tier customers. Failover is manual by
    design; see Fleet Recovery Runbook section 5.

Breaking changes

  - The 4.1 task assignment webhook payload changed shape. Custom integrations
    require updating; the generic REST/CSV adapter handles the change
    transparently.
  - Minimum HX-200 firmware raised to 3.7.0.
  - The legacy dock assignment configuration keys were removed. Sites upgrading
    from 4.1 must reconfigure charge thresholds using the new schema. Default
    charge threshold remains 20 percent and default charge target remains 90
    percent.


=============================================================================
UPGRADE GUIDANCE
=============================================================================

From 4.1.x
  Read the breaking changes in the 4.2.0 section. Reconfigure charge thresholds.
  Verify custom webhook integrations. Upgrade HX-200 firmware to 3.8.2 or later
  before or during the Fleet OS upgrade — do not leave the fleet on 3.8.1.

From 4.2.0 or 4.2.1
  Upgrade to 4.2.3 immediately. Confirm HX-200 firmware is 3.8.2 or later across
  the entire fleet, not just a subset. During INC-2024-017 the failure emerged
  eleven days after the firmware rollout completed, accumulating at two to five
  units per day while excess dock capacity absorbed it. Partial exposure is
  still exposure; it simply takes longer to surface.

From 4.2.2
  Upgrade to 4.2.3 at normal cadence. No urgency.

All upgrades
  Fleet OS restart causes a planning pause of 20 to 40 seconds. Robots hold
  position. Durable state is preserved and robots do not need re-homing.
  Schedule during a shift boundary where possible.

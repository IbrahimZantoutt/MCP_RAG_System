1. WHY THIS POLICY EXISTS

Helix commits to a 1-hour critical response, 24x7, for Enterprise customers, and
4 hours for Professional. Meeting those commitments requires people to be
available outside normal hours. Being available outside normal hours is work,
and it is compensated as work.

This policy was substantially revised in October 2024. The revisions are a direct
result of what the September 2024 incident revealed about the previous version,
which under-compensated exactly the situation it most needed to cover. Section 8
sets out what changed and why.


2. WHO IS ON CALL

Three rotations operate independently:

  Engineering       Fleet Software, Firmware, and Platform. Primary and
                    secondary. One-week shifts, Monday 09:00 to Monday 09:00
                    Austin time.
  Field Operations  Regional coverage across North America and EMEA. One-week
                    shifts.
  Support           Follow-the-sun coverage between Austin and Rotterdam, plus a
                    weekend on-call rotation.

Engineers join the engineering rotation after approximately three months, once
they have shipped meaningfully and completed the Fleet Recovery Runbook
walkthrough with their buddy. Nobody goes on call without having walked through
section 4 of that runbook — the charge dock reservation deadlock procedure — in
the simulator.


3. RESPONSE COMMITMENTS

  Engineering primary       15 minutes for SEV-1, 1 hour for SEV-2
  Engineering secondary     30 minutes when the primary has not acknowledged
  Field Operations          30 minutes for SEV-1
  Support                   Per the tier commitment: 1 hour Enterprise, 4 hours
                            Professional

Being on call means being reachable and able to begin work within your response
window. It does not mean being at a desk. It does mean not being unreachable,
not being impaired, and not being more than a reasonable distance from a working
internet connection.

Alcohol during an on-call shift is prohibited under the employee handbook. This
is a safety policy and it is not negotiable.


4. STANDBY COMPENSATION

Paid for being available, regardless of whether anything happens.

  Engineering primary, weekday night      120 USD per night
  Engineering primary, weekend day        260 USD per day
  Engineering secondary                   50 percent of primary rate
  Field Operations                        140 USD per night, 280 USD per
                                          weekend day
  Support weekend rotation                240 USD per day

  Public holiday standby                  200 percent of the equivalent rate

A full one-week engineering primary rotation with no incidents pays 1,120 USD
standby (5 weeknights at 120 USD plus 2 weekend days at 260 USD).

Standby is paid regardless of activity. It compensates the constraint on your
life, not the work performed.


5. ACTIVATION COMPENSATION

Paid when you are actually working an incident, in addition to standby.

  Hourly activation rate            1.5x your notional hourly base rate
  Minimum activation                1 hour, even for a 10-minute page
  Overnight activation              2.0x for work between 22:00 and 06:00 local
  Public holiday activation         2.0x

Activation time starts when you are paged and ends when you stop working the
incident. It includes investigation, coordination, customer calls, and immediate
write-up. It does not include the full postmortem, which is normal working time.

Non-exempt employees are paid per applicable overtime law where that produces a
higher figure. This policy never reduces a statutory entitlement.


6. EXTENDED INCIDENT PROVISIONS

These are the provisions people most often fail to claim. Read this section.

6.1  Extended incident premium
     Any single incident where you work more than 4 continuous hours pays an
     additional 1.0x on all hours beyond the fourth. A 7-hour incident therefore
     pays 1.5x on the first 4 hours and 2.5x on the remaining 3.

6.2  Mandatory rest
     Anyone working an incident for more than 6 continuous hours, or past
     02:00 local, takes a minimum 10-hour rest period before returning to any
     work. This is mandatory and it is the manager's responsibility to enforce,
     not the employee's to request.

     Rest is paid at normal rate. It is not PTO, it is not unpaid, and it does
     not come out of anyone's leave balance.

6.3  Recovery day
     Any incident exceeding 6 hours, or any incident requiring work past
     02:00 local, entitles the responder to a paid recovery day taken within
     the following 14 days. Not deducted from PTO. Automatically granted; no
     approval needed. Your manager is responsible for making sure you take it.

6.4  Handoff obligation
     Incidents exceeding 8 hours must be handed off to another responder.
     Nobody works an incident for more than 8 hours in a single stretch. If no
     handoff is available, that is an escalation to the group lead, not a
     reason to continue.

6.5  Travel during incidents
     Incident response travel requires no pre-approval of any kind. Book the
     flight, take the taxi, get the hotel, go. Accommodation caps do not apply.
     See the Travel and Expense Policy section 7. Travel time during an active
     incident counts as activation time.


7. WORKED EXAMPLE

An engineer is primary on-call over a weekend. On Saturday they are paged at
07:15 and work the incident until 12:59, then spend 40 minutes on the immediate
write-up.

  Standby, Saturday                                          260.00 USD
  Activation, 07:15 to 13:39 = 6.4 hours
    First 4.0 hours at 1.5x
    Remaining 2.4 hours at 2.5x (extended incident premium)
  Recovery day                                               1 paid day, taken
                                                             within 14 days
  Mandatory rest                                             Not triggered
                                                             (incident ended
                                                             before 6 continuous
                                                             hours past 02:00)

The engineer is also entitled to a recovery day under 6.3, since the incident
exceeded 6 hours from page to resolution.

This example is deliberately modelled on INC-2024-017, the Cardinal Foods charge
dock deadlock of 2024-09-14, which ran from 06:12 to 12:59 with the on-call
engineer paged at 07:15.

Under the 2023 policy, that engineer would have received standby of 180 USD plus
6.4 hours at a flat 1.25x, with no extended incident premium, no recovery day,
and no mandatory rest provision. Several people worked well past 12 hours that
day between the incident itself and the emergency hotfix that followed, and the
old policy had nothing whatever to say about it.


8. WHAT CHANGED IN THE 2024 REVISION

  - Extended incident premium added (6.1). The old policy paid a flat rate
    regardless of duration, which meant a 7-hour incident and a 30-minute page
    were compensated on the same curve.
  - Mandatory rest added (6.2). Previously there was nothing. During
    INC-2024-017 and the four days of hotfix work that followed, several
    engineers worked hours that in hindsight nobody should have approved.
  - Recovery day added (6.3), automatic rather than requested. The old policy
    had a comp-time provision that required manager approval, and almost nobody
    used it, because asking for time off after a bad weekend feels like
    admitting weakness. Making it automatic removed that.
  - Handoff obligation added (6.4).
  - Standby rates increased by approximately 30 percent across all rotations.
  - Explicit statement that incident travel needs no pre-approval (6.5),
    mirroring the Travel and Expense Policy change made for the same reason.

The underlying observation from September 2024 was simple. We asked people to
work an extremely hard Saturday and then four intense days of remediation, and
our compensation policy treated it as a slightly long page. The response was
excellent and the people involved did not complain. That is exactly why the
policy needed fixing — good people absorbing a badly designed policy is not a
sustainable operating model.


9. MANAGER OBLIGATIONS

  - Ensure rotations are staffed at least 4 weeks in advance.
  - Never assign a rotation to someone who has not completed the runbook
    walkthrough.
  - Enforce mandatory rest actively. Do not wait to be asked.
  - Confirm recovery days are actually taken within 14 days.
  - Review rotation load quarterly. Anyone consistently activated more than
    twice per rotation indicates a reliability problem to be fixed in the
    product, not an on-call staffing problem to be absorbed by people.
  - Ensure claims are submitted. The most common failure mode of this policy is
    people not claiming what they are owed, and that is a manager's problem to
    solve.


10. CLAIMS

Submit through the People Ops portal within 30 days, referencing the incident
identifier. Standby is paid automatically with the rotation; activation and
extended incident premiums must be claimed.

Payment is made with the following month's payroll.

Questions on interpretation go to People Ops. Disputes over whether time counts
as activation are resolved in favour of the employee.

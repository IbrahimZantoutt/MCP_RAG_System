1. THE ROLE

Field technicians install, commission, maintain, and repair Helix robots at
customer sites. There are 41 certified field technicians as of December 2024,
based out of Austin, Reno, and Rotterdam, covering 38 customer sites.

The job is genuinely mixed: mechanical work, electrical work, software
diagnostics, and — more than most people expect — customer relationship
management. A technician on site during a bad week is the face of Helix, and the
way they conduct themselves matters as much as the repair.

Field technicians are the only Helix employees routinely authorized to enter a
live robot operating area, and the only ones permitted to replace safety-rated
components. Both privileges carry the certification requirements below.


2. CERTIFICATION PROGRAMME

Four levels. Each requires the previous one.

FT-1  FOUNDATION                                                    2 weeks
  Safety and floor protocol, robot mechanical familiarization, basic diagnostics,
  battery handling, tool use, documentation practice.
  Authorizes: supervised site work only.

FT-2  CERTIFIED TECHNICIAN                                          4 weeks
  Installation and commissioning, dock installation, mechanical repair, drive
  train service, battery replacement including the cycle-count transfer
  procedure, fault code diagnosis across the full code set, Operator Console
  operation.
  Authorizes: unsupervised site work, all non-safety-rated component service.
  Requires: 120 hours supervised field time after FT-1.

FT-3  SENIOR TECHNICIAN                                             3 weeks
  Safety-rated component replacement and verification, safety scanner chain
  service, e-stop chain diagnosis, HX-450 hydraulic and lift systems, facility
  map field verification, Fleet OS appliance service.
  Authorizes: safety-rated component replacement, incident response lead on site.
  Requires: 12 months at FT-2 and a Safety and Compliance assessment.

FT-4  MASTER TECHNICIAN / TRAINER                                   2 weeks
  Training delivery, certification assessment, complex multi-system diagnosis,
  new site commissioning lead, customer operator training delivery.
  Authorizes: certifying other technicians, leading new site deployments.
  Requires: 24 months at FT-3.

Current distribution: 8 FT-1, 19 FT-2, 11 FT-3, 3 FT-4.

Recertification
  Annual for all levels. One day, covering the year's firmware and Fleet OS
  changes, any new fault codes, safety refresher, and incident lessons. The 2024
  recertification cycle covered INC-2024-017 in detail, because the diagnostic
  signature — robots accumulating at chargers with no fault codes and every
  health indicator green — is precisely the kind of thing technicians are
  trained not to look for.

  Lapsed certification suspends site authorization immediately. There is no
  grace period and no exception, including for scheduled work.


3. FLOOR ENTRY PROTOCOL

This is the most important section in this manual and it is delivered verbatim
to customer facility staff.

Nobody enters a robot operating area without completing every step. This applies
to technicians, engineers, executives, customer staff, and visitors. There is no
exemption for seniority, for a short visit, or for being in a hurry.

  3.1  Notify the facility operator. They must know you are entering and where.

  3.2  Pause the fleet, or lock out the zone. For work confined to one area,
       zone lockout is preferred — it keeps the rest of the facility running.
       For anything ambiguous, pause the whole fleet.

  3.3  Verify the pause took effect. Confirm on the Operator Console that robots
       in the zone are stopped. Do not rely on visual observation alone; a robot
       that appears stationary may simply be between tasks.

  3.4  Carry a wireless emergency stop pendant. Always. Test it before entry.

  3.5  Wear high-visibility clothing and safety footwear.

  3.6  Enter with a second person present, or with a facility operator watching
       from the console who has confirmed they are watching. Do not enter a live
       area alone.

  3.7  On exit, confirm the area is clear of people and tools before releasing
       the lockout or resuming the fleet. Tools left on the floor are a
       frequent cause of E-704 drive motor overcurrent and E-118 payload shift
       events.

  3.8  Record entry and exit. Every entry is logged.

If any step cannot be completed, do not enter. There is no situation on a
customer floor urgent enough to justify skipping this protocol. A degraded fleet
costs money; a person struck by a 340 kg Stacker does not have a cost that
matters in the same units.


4. SAFETY PRINCIPLES TECHNICIANS MUST KNOW

  4.1  Safety lives in firmware, not in Fleet OS. Fleet OS tells a robot where to
       go; firmware decides how and retains authority to refuse. Fleet OS cannot
       override an emergency stop, disable obstacle detection, or command motion
       outside the certified envelope. There is no service mode that relaxes
       this, no debug flag, and no customer request that changes it.

       The practical implication for you on site: a Fleet OS problem, however
       severe, cannot make a robot move unsafely. During INC-2024-017 the fleet
       controller deadlocked 61 robots for nearly seven hours with zero safety
       consequence. Robots sat still. That is what the architecture is for.

  4.2  Fail static. A confused robot stops and holds. If you find a robot
       stationary with no obvious cause, treat it as a robot that has correctly
       decided not to move rather than a robot that has failed.

  4.3  Never bypass a safety function. Not for diagnosis, not for a demo, not
       for five minutes. Disabling the safety scanner chain, disabling the
       dynamic speed envelope, raising a speed limit, or removing e-stop
       hardware voids certification and is grounds for termination.

  4.4  Safety-rated components are FT-3 work only. Safety scanner replacement
       requires FT-3 certification and post-replacement verification. Customer
       staff must never replace them regardless of their own technical skill.

  4.5  An e-stop that will not clear after physical reset indicates a fault in
       the e-stop chain. Take the robot out of service immediately and escalate.
       This is safety-relevant and reportable to Safety and Compliance within
       24 hours.

  4.6  Report every near-miss. Any contact between a robot and a person however
       minor, any unexpected motion, any safety function that did not behave as
       designed. Within 24 hours, to Safety and Compliance. Reporting is never
       held against you, and any manager who implies otherwise is in breach of
       the employee handbook.


5. COMMON FIELD PROCEDURES

Battery replacement
  Follow the procedure exactly, including the cycle-count transfer utility.
  Replacing the battery management board without running it resets cumulative
  charge cycle count and destroys the replacement history for that unit (known
  issue FW-1163). Rated life is 4,000 cycles to 80 percent capacity, roughly
  five years at typical duty, and that history is what the predictive
  replacement model on the 2025 roadmap will depend on.

Safety scanner cleaning and replacement
  Cleaning is FT-1 work and resolves most E-207 occlusion faults. Replacement is
  FT-3 only. Sites with high airborne dust — Cardinal Foods Memphis in
  particular — need cleaning on a scheduled basis rather than reactively.

Dock service
  Handshake failures on docks running firmware older than 1.4 are a known issue
  (FW-1142). Update dock firmware before diagnosing further. Two docks at
  Brightline Apparel Reno are currently affected.

Facility map field verification
  FT-3 and above. Note carefully that maps are edited in a desktop tool and
  imported as an opaque binary blob with no diff, no review, and no rollback. A
  bad map import is a site-down event with no undo. Verify twice. Map lifecycle
  management is on the 2025 roadmap.

Fleet OS appliance service
  FT-3 and above. Always capture diagnostics before restarting anything —
  helixctl diag capture --full — because a restart destroys the in-memory floor
  state that will be needed to explain what happened. Full procedure is in the
  Fleet Recovery Runbook section 2.

Diagnostic capture handling
  A full capture can contain customer business data. Store it in the support
  system against the relevant ticket. Never on a personal machine, never on a
  shared drive, never attached to email. This is covered in the Data Handling
  and Privacy Policy and it is not a formality.


6. WORKING WITH CUSTOMERS

You are frequently on site during a bad day. Some things that matter:

  - Say what you know and what you do not. Customers detect hedging immediately
    and it costs more trust than admitting uncertainty.
  - Never speculate about SLA credits. That conversation belongs to the account
    team, always. A well-meaning technician suggesting a customer might be owed
    a credit creates a commitment the company may not be able to honour.
  - Never share internal postmortems. Customer-facing summaries are prepared
    separately and approved by the account team.
  - Do not blame the customer, even when the cause is on their side. E-330 WMS
    sync failures are usually caused by customer-side credential or certificate
    expiry, and the goal is to get their IT team engaged quickly, not to win the
    argument about whose fault it is.
  - Escalate rather than improvise. Every runbook procedure has a time budget.
    When it expires, escalate. Nobody has ever been criticized here for
    escalating too early.


7. ON-CALL AND COMPENSATION

Field Operations runs its own on-call rotation with regional coverage across
North America and EMEA. One-week shifts, 30-minute response for SEV-1.

Compensation is covered by the On-Call Compensation Policy, which was
substantially revised in October 2024. Provisions technicians most often fail to
claim:

  - Extended incident premium: an additional 1.0x on all hours beyond the fourth
    of a single continuous incident
  - Automatic paid recovery day for any incident exceeding 6 hours, or requiring
    work past 02:00 local, not deducted from PTO
  - Mandatory 10-hour paid rest after 6 continuous hours or work past 02:00,
    which your manager is responsible for enforcing rather than you for
    requesting

Incident travel requires no pre-approval whatsoever. Book the flight, take the
taxi, get the hotel, go. Accommodation caps do not apply. Travel time during an
active incident counts as activation time. This rule exists because in September
2024 a field engineer lost hours chasing approval for a Saturday fare during a
critical outage at our largest customer, and that will not happen again.

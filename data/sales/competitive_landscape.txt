1. MARKET POSITION

Helix competes in indoor autonomous mobile robots for distribution and
fulfillment. We are a mid-size player: 1,510 robots deployed, 38 sites, 33.2
million USD ARR. We are neither the volume leader nor the technology leader by
raw capability. Where we win is the combination of mixed-traffic safety
certification, on-premise architecture, and operational transparency.

Win rate in competitive deals, 2024: 41 percent overall. 58 percent in deals
where mixed human-robot floor operation is a stated requirement. 12 percent in
deals involving high-bay racking, which is a segment we effectively do not serve.


2. PRINCIPAL COMPETITORS

VERTEX LOGISTICS SYSTEMS
  The volume leader in North America. Roughly 9,000 units deployed. Well
  capitalized, broad product line including high-bay.

  Strengths
    - Scale and reference density. Almost every prospect has heard of them.
    - Full product range including high-bay, which we cannot answer.
    - Aggressive pricing; they will go below our floor and can absorb it.

  Weaknesses we observe consistently
    - Cloud-dependent control plane. Their fleet controller requires connectivity
      to their cloud for task orchestration. Several prospects have told us about
      site outages caused by internet or cloud availability rather than by
      anything in the facility. This is our single strongest differentiator.
    - Availability commitments defined loosely. Their standard agreement counts a
      robot as available if it is powered and connected. Ours, since 2024-10-01,
      requires eligibility for dispatch.
    - Slower on safety certification for genuinely mixed-traffic environments.
      Several of their deployments segregate robots from people by physical
      barrier, which is a different product category.

  Where they beat us
    High-bay. Price. Deals where the buyer is optimizing for lowest cost per
    unit and does not have a mixed-traffic safety requirement.

ORBIT ROBOTICS
  Venture-backed, fast-moving, strong perception technology. Roughly 2,200 units.

  Strengths
    - Genuinely excellent perception. Their obstacle classification is ahead of
      Helix Vision and we should be honest about that internally.
    - Slick product experience. Their operator interface demos extremely well.
    - Fast release cadence.

  Weaknesses
    - Reliability at scale. Their largest deployment is under 100 units; we run
      240 at Cardinal Foods Memphis on a single instance. Prospects above 150
      units should be asked directly what Orbit's largest reference is.
    - Fast release cadence cuts both ways. Regulated customers find their change
      velocity difficult; Voss Pharma evaluated them and eliminated them on
      exactly this basis.
    - Thin support organization. No named TAM equivalent.

  Where they beat us
    Perception-heavy requirements. Smaller, design-conscious buyers. Deals where
    the demo decides.

KRONOS INTRALOGISTICS
  European, established, conservative. Strong in DACH and Benelux. Roughly 4,500
  units, mostly in Europe.

  Strengths
    - Deep European presence and regulatory familiarity. CE, EU data residency,
      and works-council dynamics are native to them.
    - Strong SSO, identity, and audit capability. This is where they beat us
      today and it is why they are a live threat at Voss Pharma expansion.
    - Long track record; low perceived risk.

  Weaknesses
    - Older architecture. Slower to adopt modern perception.
    - Weak in North America; effectively no presence.
    - Rigid. Customization and integration work is slow and expensive.

  Where they beat us
    EMEA regulated accounts. Any deal where identity governance and audit are
    gating. This is precisely the Voss Pharma Frankfurt and Dublin situation.


3. WHERE WE WIN

  - Mixed human-robot floor operation with a real safety requirement. Our
    ISO 3691-4 certification, dual safety-rated scanner chain at IEC 61508 SIL 2,
    and firmware-authoritative safety architecture are genuinely differentiated.
    Fleet OS cannot override an e-stop, disable obstacle detection, or command
    motion outside the certified envelope, and that is not configurable. Buyers
    with a safety officer in the room understand what that means.
  - On-premise control. A network partition cannot stop robots from working.
    Against Vertex specifically this closes deals.
  - Scale references. 240 units on one instance at Cardinal Foods is a number
    Orbit cannot match and that Vertex will not quote a comparable single-
    instance figure for.
  - Operational transparency. Covered in section 5; it is becoming our most
    distinctive asset and it came out of our worst incident.


4. WHERE WE LOSE

  - High-bay racking. 12 percent win rate. Neither the HX-200 nor the HX-450
    reaches above 2,400 mm. Scope deals to what we can service and say so
    plainly; the HX-700 concept is a feasibility study with a July 2025 decision
    gate and must never be represented as planned.
  - Identity governance and audit. Operator Console SSO does not exist. This
    blocks approximately 4.4 million USD of pipeline, of which 2.6 million USD is
    Voss Pharma. Kronos wins these today. Roadmap item 3.1, H1 2025, protected.
  - Lowest-price deals. Our gross margin was 51 percent in Q3 2024 and our floor
    is 38 percent. Vertex will go lower than we should.
  - EU data residency. Telemetry exports to US regions. Becoming a gating
    requirement in EMEA. Roadmap H2 2025.


5. OPERATIONAL TRANSPARENCY AS A DIFFERENTIATOR

This is worth its own section because it is counterintuitive and because it has
started winning deals.

After INC-2024-017 — the September 2024 charge dock deadlock that cost Cardinal
Foods 6 hours 47 minutes and cost us 148,000 USD in credits — we did four things
that competitors generally do not:

  1. Delivered a technically complete customer-facing postmortem naming the
     actual mechanism, three days inside our five-day commitment.
  2. Sent the CTO on site to walk the customer's operations and IT leads through
     the real root cause rather than a sanitized version.
  3. Changed our own SLA definition against our own interest, because the old
     definition let a total operational failure measure as near-perfect
     availability.
  4. Shipped the fix in four days and the process remediation within eight weeks.

Denise Okafor at Cardinal Foods summarized it as: "You broke it, you told us
exactly how, you fixed it in four days, and you changed your own contract terms
because of it. That is more than I get from most vendors."

We now use this deliberately. In competitive evaluations against Vertex, asking a
prospect to request their most recent customer-facing postmortem and their
precise availability definition is an extremely effective question, because
Vertex's answer to both is weak.

Handle with care. This works only if we are honest about the incident, including
that our monitoring did not detect it for 41 minutes and a customer shift
supervisor telephoned us. Presenting it as a triumph rather than as a failure we
handled well destroys the effect entirely.


6. APPROVED POSITIONING LANGUAGE

Use these formulations. Do not assert competitor weaknesses as fact; ask
questions that let the prospect discover them.

On architecture
  "Fleet OS runs entirely on-premise. If your internet connection fails, your
  robots keep working. It is worth asking any vendor what happens to their fleet
  during a cloud outage."

On safety
  "Our robots are certified for continuous shared operation with your people
  under ISO 3691-4. Safety is enforced in robot firmware, not in the fleet
  controller, and cannot be overridden by software. Ask what a competitor's fleet
  controller is able to override."

On availability
  "Our availability commitment counts a robot as available only if it is
  eligible to do work. We changed that definition ourselves in October 2024 after
  an incident showed us the old one was too weak. Ask other vendors for their
  exact definition in writing."

On scale
  "We run 240 robots on a single instance at our largest customer. It is fair to
  ask any vendor for their largest single-site production reference and the
  number of units in it."

On high-bay
  "We do not serve high-bay racking today, and I would rather tell you that now
  than scope a deal we deliver badly."

On transparency
  "Ask us for a postmortem from a real incident. Then ask our competitors for
  one."

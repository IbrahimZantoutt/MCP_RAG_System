WHAT MAKES THIS ACCOUNT DIFFERENT

Change control
  Voss Pharma does not take upgrades on our cadence. Every version is assessed
  against their validation framework before deployment. A typical Fleet OS
  minor version takes 6 to 10 weeks from our release to their production.

  This is why Basel runs the HX-200 3.6.x LTS firmware line rather than current
  stable. 3.6.x is supported until 2026-06-30 and receives security and critical
  fixes only, which is precisely what their validation process prefers: a slow-
  moving, well-characterized baseline.

  A consequence worth noting: Basel was never exposed to INC-2024-017. The
  charge dock deadlock required HX-200 firmware 3.8.1, and the 3.6.x LTS line
  never adopted the SoC hysteresis band that caused it. Anna Kowalczyk
  nonetheless required a formal assessment of the incident against their
  environment, and we backported the threshold-crossing logic into firmware
  3.6.4 (released 2024-11-19) at her request — not because 3.6.x was vulnerable,
  but because their process requires that known-relevant fixes be applied or
  formally waived, and applying it was cheaper than arguing.

  That episode is a good illustration of how this account works. It also went a
  long way with Dr. Halvorsen: we treated their process as legitimate rather than
  as an obstacle.

Audit and data controls
  Voss Pharma audits us annually. The 2024 audit was completed in October with
  two findings, both closed.

  They also required the ability to suspend outbound telemetry export during
  audit windows, which is why Fleet OS 4.2.3 gained pause-and-resume for the
  Telemetry Pipeline without a full restart. That feature exists for this
  customer.

EU data residency
  Telemetry currently exports to Helix Cloud in US regions. Voss Pharma has
  accepted this under a data processing agreement for the Basel site, but has
  stated clearly that EU data residency is a requirement for any expansion. This
  is on the 2025 roadmap under EMEA deployment tooling (H2 2025).


THE SSO BLOCKER

This is the defining issue on the account and the reason expansion is stalled.

The Fleet OS Operator Console does not support single sign-on. Authentication is
local accounts managed within Fleet OS. Mathieu Perrin has stated in writing
that this is non-compliant with Voss Pharma's identity governance standard,
which requires that all systems with production operational control authenticate
against their central identity provider with enforced MFA and centralized
deprovisioning.

Basel operates today under a documented compensating control: a quarterly manual
account reconciliation performed jointly by Stefan Bruhn's team and Mathieu
Perrin's. This is an exception with an expiry, and Mathieu has been explicit that
it will not be renewed indefinitely.

Consequences
  - Frankfurt (approximately 90 units, 1.6 million USD) is blocked pending SSO.
    Site assessment is complete and the business case is approved on their side.
  - Dublin (approximately 55 units, 1.0 million USD) is behind Frankfurt.
  - Renewal risk at 2026-06-30 is real if SSO has not shipped. Dr. Halvorsen has
    not threatened this and would not; Mathieu Perrin's compensating control
    expiry does it implicitly.

SAML and OIDC SSO, RBAC refinement, and a tamper-evident audit log are on the
2025 roadmap as item 3.1, scheduled H1 2025. It is identified in the roadmap as
the highest-confidence revenue unlock available and is explicitly protected from
reallocation. Elena Vasquez has committed to Dr. Halvorsen that we will deliver
in H1 2025. That commitment is real and should not be undermined by quietly
letting the item slip.

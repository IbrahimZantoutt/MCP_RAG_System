PURPOSE

The HX-200 Courier is 78 percent of units shipped and the majority of hardware
revenue. Company blended gross margin is 51 percent and hardware gross margin is
34 percent, so HX-200 economics substantially determine company economics.

This analysis establishes where the cost sits, what a robot earns over its life,
and what the H2 2025 hardware revision D cost reduction is actually worth.

Helix Vision is the clearest opportunity in this entire analysis. It carries 61
percent margin, it materially improves obstacle handling and reduces
conservative stops, and it attaches on fewer than one unit in three. Every
percentage point of attach rate improvement across TTM volume is worth
approximately 43,000 USD of annual gross profit. Moving attach from 31 to 50
percent would add roughly 820,000 USD.

The 2025 roadmap addresses this two ways: reducing module cost, and surfacing the
capability in the Operator Console for non-Vision fleets so customers can see
what they are missing rather than being sold it abstractly.


3. RECURRING REVENUE PER UNIT

This is where the business actually works.

  Fleet OS licence, Professional          180 USD per robot per month
  Fleet OS licence, Enterprise            265 USD per robot per month
  Weighted average realized (TTM)         214 USD per robot per month

  Annual recurring revenue per robot      2,568 USD
  Cost to serve per robot per year          308 USD
  Gross profit per robot per year         2,260 USD
  Software gross margin                      88 percent

Cost to serve comprises support staffing, field operations allocation, cloud
telemetry infrastructure, and a warranty and SLA credit reserve. The reserve was
raised from 190 to 308 USD per robot per year in the Q4 2024 revision, for two
reasons: the actual credit experience of Q3 2024, and the revised SLA definition
effective 2024-10-01, which requires dispatch eligibility for a robot to count as
available and therefore increases credit exposure by an estimated 120,000 to
200,000 USD annually across the fleet.


4. LIFETIME VALUE PER UNIT

Assumptions: 5-year service life, matching expected battery service life at
4,000 rated cycles under typical duty. 92 percent annual logo retention. No
churn assumed within a deployed fleet, which reflects actual experience — we have
never had a customer reduce a deployed fleet size.

  Hardware gross profit, at sale                       6,885 USD
  Options gross profit (weighted by attach)            2,940 USD
  Software gross profit, 5 years                      11,300 USD
  Services gross profit, allocated                       620 USD
  Total gross profit per unit over 5 years            21,745 USD

  Customer acquisition cost, allocated per unit        3,910 USD
  Deployment cost, allocated per unit                  1,240 USD
  Net lifetime value per unit                         16,595 USD

  Payback period on fully loaded cost                 14 months

The structure is unambiguous. Hardware gross profit at sale is 6,885 USD; software
gross profit over the same five years is 11,300 USD. Software is 52 percent of
lifetime gross profit from a product that is, at the point of sale, a hardware
purchase.

This is the arithmetic behind the rule that software licence discounting requires
CFO approval and is essentially never granted. Trading recurring high-margin
software revenue to win a one-time hardware price negotiation is the worst trade
available to this company, and it is a trade that feels reasonable in the room.


5. HARDWARE REVISION D — PROJECTED IMPACT

Targeted for H2 2025. Objective is a 14 percent component cost reduction with no
change to the certified safety envelope.

  Drive board integration (merge drive electronics
    and compute carrier)                             (1,340) USD
  Single-vendor LiDAR strategy (replace dual-source
    navigation and safety LiDAR arrangement)         (1,620) USD
  Chassis part-count reduction                         (780) USD
  Harness simplification                               (410) USD
  Battery pack cost (renegotiated supply)              (290) USD
  Projected component cost reduction                 (4,440) USD

  Revised component cost                             26,110 USD
  Revised cost of goods sold                         31,855 USD
  Gross profit per unit at current ASP               11,325 USD
  Gross margin per unit                                 26 percent

A move from 16 to 26 percent on the base unit. At TTM HX-200 volume this is worth
approximately 2.6 million USD of annual gross profit, which is the single largest
identified margin improvement available to the company.

Principal risk: the single-vendor LiDAR change touches the safety scanner chain
and requires IEC 61508 SIL 2 re-assessment. Re-assessment timelines are set by
the certification body and are outside our control. If re-assessment slips,
revision D slips with it and the 1,620 USD LiDAR saving — the largest single line
— is the piece at risk.

Mitigation is sequencing: Nikolai Petrov and the certification body are engaged
from Q1 2025 rather than at design freeze. This is explicit in the 2025 roadmap.


6. SENSITIVITY

  Each 1 percent of realized ASP           ~432 USD gross profit per unit
  Each 1 pp of Vision attach rate          ~43,000 USD annual gross profit
  Each 10 USD of monthly licence price     ~600 USD lifetime gross profit
                                            per unit
  Each 1 pp of logo retention              ~340 USD lifetime value per unit

Price realization on hardware is the most sensitive single lever and the one
most exposed to sales discretion. At 16 percent base-unit margin, a 5 percent
discount consumes roughly 2,160 USD of a 6,885 USD gross profit — nearly a third
— for a single point of negotiating leverage.

The discount floor of 38 percent blended gross margin exists for this reason and
should be treated as a genuine floor rather than an opening position.

HELIX ROBOTICS — TRAVEL AND EXPENSE POLICY
Owner: Sam Delacroix, Chief Financial Officer
Administered by: Wei-Lin Tsai (Finance), Priya Raghunathan (People Ops)
Effective: 2024-07-01
Last revised: 2024-11-04
Applies to: All employees and contractors
Classification: Internal


1. PRINCIPLES

Three rules govern everything below.

  Spend the company's money as you would spend your own, if you were reasonably
  well off and in a hurry.

  If you would be uncomfortable explaining an expense to a colleague, do not
  incur it.

  Getting a customer's fleet working takes precedence over this policy. Field
  Operations and on-call engineering responding to a SEV-1 do not need
  pre-approval for anything. Book the flight, take the taxi, get there. Explain
  afterward. Section 7 covers this explicitly.

This policy is deliberately short. It is not an attempt to enumerate every case.


2. APPROVAL THRESHOLDS

  Under 200 USD                    No pre-approval. Submit with receipt.
  200 to 1,000 USD                 Manager approval.
  1,000 to 5,000 USD               Director approval.
  5,000 to 25,000 USD              VP approval.
  Above 25,000 USD                 CFO approval.

  Any single expense above 500 USD requires an itemized receipt regardless of
  category.


3. AIR TRAVEL

  Economy class for flights under 6 hours. Premium economy permitted for flights
  over 6 hours. Business class requires VP approval and is generally reserved for
  overnight transatlantic travel where the traveller works the following day.

  Book at least 14 days in advance where the trip is foreseeable. Fares booked
  inside 7 days require a note explaining why, except for incident response
  travel (section 7), which requires no explanation.

  Preferred booking through the corporate travel tool. Direct booking is
  permitted where it is cheaper or where the travel tool cannot handle the
  itinerary, which is common for Rotterdam-originating EMEA travel.

  Airline loyalty points belong to the traveller. Choosing a materially more
  expensive flight to accrue them does not.


4. ACCOMMODATION

  Nightly caps, excluding tax:

    Austin, Nashville, Columbus, Reno       220 USD
    Memphis                                 190 USD
    San Francisco, New York, Boston         340 USD
    Basel, Zurich, Frankfurt                310 CHF / EUR equivalent
    Rotterdam, Amsterdam                    260 EUR
    Dublin                                  270 EUR
    All other locations                     240 USD

  Exceeding a cap requires manager approval and a one-line reason. Conference
  venue rates and customer-site proximity are both acceptable reasons.

  Extended field deployments exceeding 14 consecutive nights should use serviced
  apartments rather than hotels. Field Operations coordinates this; it is
  materially cheaper and considerably more humane.


5. MEALS

  Per diem, or actuals with receipts, not both. Choose per trip.

    Domestic per diem                       75 USD per day
    EMEA per diem                           95 EUR per day
    Partial travel days                     60 percent of per diem

  Team meals and customer entertainment are actuals, require the attendee list
  in the expense note, and require director approval above 500 USD.

  Alcohol is reimbursable with meals in reasonable quantity. It is not
  reimbursable on customer sites, at any Helix facility with a robot test bay, or
  at any point during an on-call shift. This is a safety policy, not a
  temperance one.


6. GROUND TRANSPORT

  Rideshare and taxi are reimbursable. Rental cars require manager approval and
  are expected where a trip involves multiple customer sites in a region.

  Personal vehicle mileage is reimbursed at the current IRS standard rate for US
  travel and the equivalent statutory rate for EMEA.

  Parking and tolls are reimbursable. Traffic and parking fines are not, ever,
  under any circumstances.


7. INCIDENT RESPONSE TRAVEL

This section exists because of what happened in September 2024, and it is the
part of this policy most worth reading.

During INC-2024-017, a Helix field engineer delayed booking a same-day flight to
Memphis while attempting to reach a director for approval on a 1,400 USD fare on
a Saturday. That delay was measured in hours during an active SEV-1 at our
largest customer, and it was entirely the fault of this document as it was then
written.

The rules now:

  7.1  For any active SEV-1 or SEV-2, no pre-approval is required for any travel
       expense. Book the flight. Take the taxi. Get the hotel. Go.

  7.2  This applies to Field Operations, on-call engineering, support, and any
       named account team member. It applies at any hour on any day.

  7.3  Notify your manager when convenient. Convenient means after the customer
       situation is stable, not before departure.

  7.4  Submit the expense afterward with the incident identifier in the note.
       Incident-tagged expenses are approved by Finance directly and do not route
       through the standard chain.

  7.5  Nightly accommodation caps do not apply to incident response travel.
       Proximity to the customer site is the only relevant criterion.

  7.6  If you are ever unsure whether a situation qualifies, it qualifies. The
       cost of an unnecessary same-day flight is trivial next to the cost of an
       hour of degraded fleet at an Enterprise customer. For reference, the
       September 2024 incident cost 338,000 USD in direct costs and deferred a
       1.9 million USD expansion decision by approximately two months.

Finance will not question incident-tagged expenses on cost grounds. If a pattern
emerges suggesting the classification is being misused, I will address it as a
management matter with the individuals concerned, not by adding friction for
everyone else.


8. FIELD TECHNICIAN STANDING ARRANGEMENTS

Field technicians travelling to customer sites on a scheduled basis operate under
a standing approval covering flights, accommodation within cap, ground transport,
and per diem for the assigned site. No per-trip approval is needed.

Tools, consumables, and replacement parts are procured through Field Operations
supply channels, never on personal expense cards. The exception is genuinely
urgent local purchase during an incident, which falls under section 7.


9. NON-REIMBURSABLE

  Traffic and parking fines
  Personal entertainment, including in-flight purchases unrelated to work
  Travel insurance beyond the corporate policy, which already covers all
    business travel
  Upgrades purchased with personal funds and then expensed
  Alcohol on customer sites, at Helix test bay facilities, or during on-call
    shifts
  Expenses submitted more than 90 days after they were incurred, absent an
    explanation
  Home office equipment, which is handled separately by People Ops under the
    employee handbook


10. SUBMISSION

  Submit through the expense system within 30 days. Receipts required above
  500 USD in all categories, and for all accommodation and air travel regardless
  of amount.

  Incident-tagged expenses require the incident identifier and are routed to
  Finance directly.

  Reimbursement runs weekly. Expenses approved by Wednesday are paid the
  following Tuesday.

  Questions on interpretation go to Wei-Lin Tsai. Questions on whether something
  is reasonable go to your manager. Genuine edge cases come to me, and there are
  fewer of them than people expect.
